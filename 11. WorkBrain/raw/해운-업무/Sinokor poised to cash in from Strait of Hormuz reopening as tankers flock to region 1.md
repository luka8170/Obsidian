Sinokor is in prime position to cash in on a potential reopening of the Strait of Hormuz as shipowners and traders prepare for a potential resumption of shipments through the energy artery.

The Ga-Hyun Chung-led company, whose winter shopping spree made it the world’s largest VLCC owner, has 14 such vessels in the Gulf of Oman, several of which were repositioned there in recent days before shutting off AIS, ship tracking data shows.

Owners and charterers with VLCCs in the region stand to benefit the most when things start getting back to normal, according to Gibson Shipbrokers.

“The near-term trajectory of the VLCC market remains entirely hinged on the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz,” the UK broker said. “The ballasters already waiting in the Gulf of Oman hold first-mover advantage to capture the initial wave of resurgent mainstream flows.”

Of the 55 VLCCs in the Gulf of Oman counted by Norwegian intelligence firm Maritime Optima, Sinokor has the most.

Others with a significant presence include Greek players Thenamaris, Maran Tankers Management and Kyklades Maritime, which are in a three-way tie for second with three each.

Shipowners are not the only ones hunting a potential peace-deal payday.

Sources explain that traders control some of the VLCCs in the area, some of which were fixed on period deals before the latest boom in rates.

Tanker market experts say that while multiple players are in near identical positions with ships in the area, not everyone has the same strategy.

Some owners are awaiting Hormuz opening, while others are simply looking for employment they cannot arrange or are unwilling to drop their rate expectations.

“Every owner has a slightly different mentality,” TradeWinds is told.

Sources add that some older ships are simply stuck waiting, with a hedge between cargo from Yanbu in the Red Sea or hoping for the strait to clear.

Further building the optimism is the sight of major charterers bringing cargo to the market.

As TradeWinds has reported, Adnoc has issued its second tender in a week for tanker loadings in the Middle East Gulf beyond the restricted Strait of Hormuz.

Trade sources told Reuters that cargoes of Upper Zakum, Umm Lulu and Das crude are being offered for June to August liftings, with buyers able to bid for VLCC-sized 2m-barrel lots.

This is a sign that it is getting easier to move tankers in and out of the Gulf, while the later loadings dates could indicate optimism that a reopening of the strait can be agreed.

Kuwait is selling its first crude cargoes since the US-Iran war began in February.

Trading sources told Bloomberg that Kuwait Petroleum Corp is offering two 2m-barrel stems being carried on two VLCCs that have exited the Strait of Hormuz with AIS signals off.

Of Sinokor’s 14 VLCCs, 13 are in ballast, according to AIS data, and six have arrived in the Gulf of Oman in recent days.

Among them is the 296,800-dwt Sri Lanka Prosperity (built 2011), one of the 11 VLCCs that George Procopiou sold to Sinokor, TradeWinds reported in February.

The ship took on the new name on 1 March, according to Equasis, before sailing from China to India and then into the Gulf of Oman.

Sinokor has been a Middle East presence since the US and Israel began attacking Iran in late February.

In April, the South Korean shipping giant had 10 VLCCs in the Middle East Gulf and five on the other side of the Strait of Hormuz, which has been effectively closed for months.

As it stands, five Sinokor VLCCs remain in the Middle East Gulf.

On Monday, Clarksons had non-scrubber, non-eco VLCCs earning an average of $83,400 per day, a tick down from last Friday and 2% down week on week.

The UK broker adjusted its VLCC benchmarks in April to reflect Gulf of Oman and Red Sea loadings.
