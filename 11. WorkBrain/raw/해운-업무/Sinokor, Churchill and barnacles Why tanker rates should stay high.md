Several unusual factors are combining to support tanker rates that are already in uncharted territory for this time of year, according to Fearnley Securities.

Among them are some unwanted crustaceans, analyst Fredrik Dybwad believes.

Strength in spot markets has continued, despite the closure of the Strait of Hormuz dragging on into the summer.

VLCC rates are resting at around $100,000 per day in the Atlantic basin.

Dybwad called this “an exceptionally strong level” for the season. The June average from 2005 until this year is $40,000 per day.

The analyst sees supportive fundamentals accumulating.

Tankers trapped and idle in the Middle East Gulf will be experiencing a build-up of barnacles on their hulls that will “likely require cleaning and off-hire to be fully removed — which may add to inefficiencies and support the market once the strait again hopefully is open for merchant traffic”.

Talk has also begun to circulate that the Sinokor Maritime/MSC Mediterranean Shipping Co partnership is back in the market for secondhand VLCCs for the first time in several months.

This would further concentrate fixing power in fewer owners’ hands, with the two companies controlling at least 20% of the compliant VLCC fleet.

[TradeWinds reported](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/sinokor-stretches-its-lead-in-vlcc-market-with-sincere-navigation-secondhand-deal/2-1-2001642) this week that Taiwan’s Sincere Navigation had sold the scrubber-fitted 298,000-dwt Maxim (built 2011) for a very firm $82.5m, with MSC named as the buyer.

Gianluigi Aponte-controlled MSC is in the process of buying a [50% stake in Sinokor](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/official-world-s-richest-shipowner-equal-partner-with-sinokor-in-vlcc-superpower/2-1-1962410) after helping fund its massive VLCC acquisition spree since December.

Sister broker Fearnleys also noted that Sinokor and MSC are back in buying mood. It called the Maxim deal “a stonking price for an older lady”.

“Never have so many had one player to thank for so much, to paraphrase Churchill,” the brokerage added.

“Without Sinokor’s big market share and restraint in fixing we wouldn’t be even close to current levels.”

Dybwad said [the Sinokor story](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/frontline-chief-compares-sinokor-tanker-spree-to-nobu-su-s-bulker-play-20-years-ago/2-1-1952766), together with general inefficiencies, pent-up demand and concerns over energy security, are all supportive of the market and should give a basis for above-average earnings into 2027.

He said the second quarter might be the earnings peak this time around, however.

Fearnleys said VLCCs continue to disappear under the radar, with vessel position lists both sides of Suez a little short for prompt dates, which should dictate a further uptick in rates.

Capping that is lack of transparency.

“A tight supply/demand picture is one thing, but not having a pricing reference is clouding proceedings,” the Oslo shop added.

“If owners really want to push this, they will have to start leaking a bit more information — real time; if not, the assumption will be that it’s just more of the same.”

Fearnleys cited reports suggesting there is still prompt interest in loadings in Fujairah and Oman to the east of Hormuz from Adnoc in Abu Dhabi and Kuwait Petroleum Corp.

“Petrobras keeps the flame burning from the Atlantic, with almost daily cargo quotes Brazil/East,” the broker said.

The Maxim has been acquired for forward delivery in the first quarter of 2027.

VesselsValue assesses the ship, which operates in the Tankers International VLCC pool, as worth $79.38m.

Sincere said it will book a profit of $9.82m from the deal.

The company received valuations from London brokers Arrow at $82m and Clarksons at $81m.

In March, [Sincere confirmed the sale](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/sinokor-maritime-snaps-up-vlcc-from-sincere-navigation/2-1-1967784) of the 296,714-dwt VLCC Kondor (built 2012) for $78.4m. Sinokor was linked to this ship as well.

Sinokor’s purchase drive has seen dozens of ships change hands and asset values spike.

VesselsValue lists 80 VLCCs in its fleet, with 14 large tankers still to be delivered.
