## 4. Structural Safety — Damage Stability & Hull Strength (Core Discussion)

정 전무 raised a substantive and detailed concern about structural adequacy rules for large bulk carriers, including Newcastlemax and similar VBC (Very Large Bulk Carrier) types. This was the most technically intensive portion of the meeting.

### 4.1 Background — Polaris' Experience with Structural Issues

- Polaris has extensive experience with bulk carrier casualties and near-misses, including:  
  - **Vale Beijing** — structural failure (buckling)  
  - **Vale Indonesia** — grounding  
  - **Stellar Daisy** — loss (Polaris-operated at time)  
  - **Stellar Banner** — grounding / total loss (VOC-class)  
- 정 전무 stated these represent the most significant casualty events in his professional experience  

### 4.2 Damage Stability — Fundamental Criticism

정 전무's core argument:

> Current damage stability rules evaluate cargo **hold flooding** from the perspective of whether a single flooded hold is "safe or unsafe" — but do not adequately assess the **overall survivability of the vessel**

Specific concerns:
- Long span between fore peak and holds creates inherent vulnerability — too wide to ensure adequate residual stability after flooding  
- Rules permit exemptions from double bottom and longitudinal subdivision when forepeak-to-hold distance exceeds a threshold — effectively waiving key safety features  
- The rule focuses on **cargo** perspective, not **human life** perspective  
- Hatch coaming: wear-down margin of only **1mm** before mandatory renewal — far below single hull tanker standards (20% wear-down allowance)  
  - Implication: virtually every vessel will require hatch coaming renewal at least once or twice in its lifetime  

### 4.3 Hull Strength — Structural Optimization Risk

- Korean-built bulkers are subject to significant **structural optimization** during design; weight reduction leads to thinner scantlings  
- Comparison cited: H-Line vessels (built to KR class with certain exemptions) show more cracking than Polaris vessels  
  - KR reportedly exempted 4th and 6th ring tanks from subdivision under SOLAS equivalency — making vessels structurally lighter but more prone to long-term deformation and cracking  
- Polaris practice: any **hair crack** on their vessels triggers immediate repair — far stricter than industry norm  
- China-built equivalent vessels show fewer cracks due to heavier scantlings / less optimization  

### 4.4 PSPC & Actual Wear-Down Margins

- PSPC (Performance Standard for Protective Coatings) ensures good coating application — improving actual wear-down condition  
- However, 정 전무 stated: nominal 20% wear-down margin was never realistic for many Polaris vessels; actual margins were already marginal from delivery  
- Risk cited: progressive weakening → sudden buckling event (analogous to Vale Beijing mode of failure)  

### 4.5 Industry & Classification Society Response

- 정 전무 has previously raised these issues with **KR, Lloyd's Register, DNV, and BV**  
- Response from IACS: insufficient number of vessels (BV) or unwillingness to engage (others)  
- **Root cause of inaction identified**:  
  - If any one classification society unilaterally raises structural requirements, owners will migrate to cheaper/less demanding societies → commercial pressure prevents reform  
  - IACS committee resists change; rule reform requires consensus across all members  
- BV HQ guest agreed with the structural logic and validated the concern  
- **Suggestion**: This topic is well-suited for discussion at **IACS** level, potentially raised through BV's representative — specifically **Olivier Carti (BV HQ, Head of Technical)**

정 전무 provided context on the Stellar Daisy investigation and its broader implications:

- Criminal and civil proceedings ongoing in Korea  
- Concern: Korean government (MOF/해수부) and KR are under intense scrutiny; structural/design root causes being overshadowed by crew/operator blame
