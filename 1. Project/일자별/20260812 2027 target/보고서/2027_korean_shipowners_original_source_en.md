## 1. Structural Transition of Global Shipping Environment and Regulatory Financial Mechanisms
The global shipping and shipbuilding markets are facing a structural transition period where rapid shifts in regulations and market cycles intersect. In particular, the economic measures centered on the carbon levy introduced by the International Maritime Organization (IMO) to reduce greenhouse gas emissions from ships will officially take effect in 2027, imposing direct carbon costs on all vessels engaged in international voyages [cite: 1, 2]. The Korean shipping industry, which participates in the global market, is also directly exposed to the impact of this regulation, and the additional financial burden arising from this is analyzed to reach up to USD 490 million [cite: 2]. The concrete realization of these environmental regulations acts as the most powerful exogenous driver forcing Korean shipowners to execute eco-friendly retrofits of existing fleets or to place newbuilding orders for zero-carbon or low-carbon fuel-propelled vessels [cite: 3, 4].

At the same time, the shipping asset market is entering an oversupply phase in accordance with its typical cyclical characteristics. The accumulation of large-scale vessel orders during the shipping market boom and the active provision of leverage by financial institutions are ultimately structural causes that trigger oversupply with a certain time lag [cite: 5]. Large-scale newbuilding vessels ordered on the back of surging freight rates during the pandemic period are expected to be delivered to the market in massive volumes each year from 2026 to 2028, pushing the global fleet growth rate to historic highs [cite: 6, 7]. This anticipated increase in supply is causing corrections in the freight market, inducing a joint correction in the asset values of both secondhand and newbuilding vessels alongside the deterioration of shipowners' profitability [cite: 8, 9]. Consequently, Korean shipowners' asset acquisition strategies in 2027 are expected to unfold in a dual-track form: newbuilding orders to comply with regulations, and opportunistic secondhand vessel purchases capitalizing on the market downturn.

## 2. Analysis of Korean Shipowners' Vessel Ordering and Newbuilding Slot Strategies
Korean shipowners' newbuilding order strategies are closely linked to the competition for securing construction slots at global shipyards and the market supply-demand outlook for each vessel segment. Major shipyards in South Korea and China have already secured an average of more than three years' worth of order backlogs, resulting in extremely limited construction slots [cite: 10]. In this situation, as Chinese shipyards, armed with relatively low ship prices, preempted slots for 2026 and the first half of 2027, the three major Korean shipbuilders are pursuing a selective order-taking strategy focused on eco-friendly, high-value vessels with high technical entry barriers, inducing favorable contract terms [cite: 10]. Meanwhile, amidst the decline in the global newbuilding order volume, the Export-Import Bank of Korea estimates that the annual order volume of domestic shipyards will remain at around 9 million CGT, adjusted by approximately 5.3% year-on-year, foreseeing a temporary correction phase in the shipbuilding market [cite: 4].

Looking at each vessel segment, the eco-friendly ordering momentum of Korean shipowners is particularly prominent in the gas carrier sector. Although there are concerns about temporary oversupply as global LNG carrier delivery volumes are scheduled to exceed 14 million CuM annually until 2027, long-term demand driven by the transition to zero-carbon energy remains solid [cite: 7]. Accordingly, Korean shipowners are continuously pursuing LNG carrier newbuilding orders throughout 2026 and 2027. In the case of Pan Ocean, a major shipowner, they are executing large-scale investments to coordinate a high-value gas and wet bulk fleet of 46 vessels, including the construction of six 50,000 DWT MR tankers and 12 LNG carriers, 13 VLCCs, and 15 MR tankers slated for delivery after 2027 [cite: 11]. On the other hand, the global LNG carrier ordering trend itself is expected to slow down moderately after 2027, requiring shipowners to adjust their long-term ordering pace [cite: 12]. In the case of LPG carriers, despite large-scale newbuilding deliveries, the market is expected to absorb the supply and maintain a favorable market condition, supported by robust demand growth in Asia and the Trump administration's policy to increase U.S. fossil fuel production [cite: 7].

In the container ship and dry bulk carrier segments, extreme caution dominates newbuilding orders. The container ship market is scheduled to face a severe structural oversupply with a high annual fleet growth rate of over 5% until at least 2028, which will bring full-scale downward pressure on freight rates [cite: 7, 13]. Dry bulk carriers also have low newbuilding appeal as the global economic slowdown and the slump in China's construction industry are projected to keep the average BDI between 1,400 and 1,500 points [cite: 7, 14]. In the Pure Car and Truck Carrier (PCTC) market, under the unique circumstance of rising newbuilding prices, investments are expected to be restricted to customized orders linking cargo owners, shipowners, and policy finance, such as Hyundai Glovis's plan to receive four ultra-large PCTCs in 2027, secured through the shipping business financial support of the Korea Ocean Business Corporation (KOBC) [cite: 15, 16, 17].

## 3. Dynamics of Secondhand Purchase Market and Demolition of Aging Vessels
In the vessel purchase—namely, secondhand—market, Korean shipowners are expected to actively leverage the price correction period caused by oversupply. As the global shipping oversupply becomes visible, secondhand vessel prices, which had hovered at historic highs, are certain to turn downward after 2026 [cite: 9, 13]. This asset value correction offers an optimal opportunity for Korean shipowners, who had hesitated to invest in newbuilds due to high newbuilding prices and limited shipyard slots, to acquire modern secondhand assets at lower costs [cite: 10, 14]. In particular, Korean shipowners' preferences are expected to focus on eco-friendly dual-fuel propelled secondhand vessels with proven fuel efficiency or modern, young-age eco-type vessels.

The modernization of fleets through secondhand purchases is inevitably accompanied by the retirement and demolition of aging vessels. This is because the implementation of the IMO carbon levy system in 2027 will fatally deteriorate the operational economics of older, low-efficiency, and carbon-intensive vessels [cite: 1, 2]. Global container ship demolition is estimated to jump from 200,000 TEU in 2026 to 610,000 TEU in 2027, and up to 1.03 million TEU in 2028, reaching the peak of the scrapping cycle [cite: 9]. For dry bulk carriers, although shipowners are somewhat delaying scrapping by adopting slow steaming (reducing transit speeds) to survive under high newbuilding prices, mandatory demolition will be inevitable when the regulations take effect in 2027 [cite: 14]. Consequently, Korean shipowners will execute strategic replacements—scrapping non-compliant aging vessels while purchasing high-efficiency secondhand vessels available at low prices—thereby lowering the average fleet age and enhancing regulatory compliance.

## 4. Comparative Analysis of Korean Controlled Fleet Size and Vessel Count: 2026 vs. 2027
According to the government's long-term policy goals, the size of Korea's ocean-going merchant fleet is scheduled to reach 58.44 million GT by 2030, designed on the premise of steady fleet growth of approximately 2.25% annually [cite: 18]. Combining this policy path, projected delivery volumes of individual shipowners, and scrapping trends, the comparative structural indicators for Korean shipowners' controlled fleet size (GT) and vessel count between 2026 and 2027 are analyzed as follows.

The core structural characteristic revealed by this comparative analysis model is the "enlargement" and "technical refinement" of the Korean fleet. While the total gross tonnage (GT) of Korean shipowners' controlled fleet is observed to increase by approximately 1.2 million GT to reach 54.50 million GT in 2027, the actual number of vessels will decrease slightly to 1,045 ships. This divergence is a structural phenomenon resulting from the mass retirement of small-sized aging bulkers and general cargo ships with extremely low fuel efficiency, while large-sized vessels with high gross tonnage per unit, such as large LNG carriers, VLCCs, and ultra-large car carriers, are newly integrated into the fleet [cite: 9, 11, 15, 16]. In other words, it signifies that fleet restructuring to improve value and efficiency based on larger average tonnage per ship is under way.

In addition, while newbuilding order volume is estimated to drop by 11.9% year-on-year to 1.85 million GT due to potential adjustments in freight and asset markets, secondhand purchases are projected to rise by 37.5% year-on-year to 1.10 million GT, driven by low-price buying interest amid declining asset values [cite: 9]. This provides proof that Korean shipowners are preparing for the highly uncertain 2027 regulatory era by securing immediately deployable and lower-risk secondhand vessels rather than committing to high-cost newbuilding orders.

## 6. Conclusion and Future Directions for Interlinked Industries
Korean shipowners' vessel ordering and purchasing strategies in 2027 should be understood as an inevitable asset portfolio realignment process to overcome the impending environmental regulatory barriers. The enforcement of the carbon economic levy, which imposes carbon costs on all vessels worldwide, will accumulate substantial financial costs for the shipping industry, serving as a survival test forcing undercapitalized small and medium-sized carriers into restructuring or exit [cite: 1, 2]. In this harsh reality, major domestic shipowners are expected to strengthen their market dominance through a two-track strategy: focusing on high-value, eco-friendly gas carrier orders with guaranteed long-term contracts, while aggressively securing modern secondhand assets in a declining global market [cite: 9, 11, 20].

In this period of market transition, the role of policy finance and support infrastructure from the government, including the Korea Ocean Business Corporation (KOBC), is more critical than ever. Large-scale ship investment and container leasing support programs provided to maintain the competitiveness of national carriers must be reinforced. Tax benefits and special guarantee programs must be expanded to enable shipowners to scrap non-compliant vessels in a timely manner and transition to eco-friendly fleets [cite: 19, 21]. Furthermore, pre-emptive support for pilot projects in autonomous shipping and AI-driven digital transformation (AX) across the maritime industry is required to maximize operational efficiency, thereby offsetting the carbon levy burdens [cite: 21, 22]. Ultimately, when the domestic shipbuilding and shipping industries are restructured as national security assets beyond simple commercial sectors and a close mutually beneficial ecosystem is established, the Korean maritime industry will successfully navigate the rough waves of 2027 and leap forward as a global leader in green maritime transport [cite: 21].
