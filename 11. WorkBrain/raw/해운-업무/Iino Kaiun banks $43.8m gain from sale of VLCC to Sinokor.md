Expanding South Korean player is said to have paid $51m for Japanese owner’s Kiho

![Iino Kaiun president Yusuke Otani.](https://image.dngroup.com/global/nhst/binary/e2cebb34507ffb6f774c599431ce69ba?crop=280%2C187%2Cx0%2Cy0%2Csafe&width=1000&format=auto&quality=80)

Iino Kaiun president Yusuke Otani.

Published 10 March 2026, 15:31

Tokyo-listed shipowner Iino Kaiun Kaisha has emerged as the latest seller of a VLCC to mega-collector Sinokor Merchant Marine.

According to shipbrokers, Sinokor paid $51m for Iino Kaiun’s 300,900-dwt crude tanker Kiho (built 2006).

Iino Kaiun, in a stock exchange filing last Friday, confirmed it had sold the ship but did not identify the buyer nor the sale price.

The company said it made a gain of ¥6.9bn ($43.8m) from the sale.

In its filing, Iino Kaiun said its board had agreed to finalise the tanker sale through its wholly owned subsidiary Dragon’s Mouth Carriers.

“We refrain from disclosing the transfer price and book value due to confidentiality obligations with the transferee,” Iino Kaiun said, adding that the sale was made at fair value and reflected market prices.

Iino Kaiun said it sold the Kiho in order “to effectively utilise management resources and improve asset efficiency”.

The company raised its full-year earnings outlook and increased its dividend despite a sharp fall in nine-month profits, boosted by the sale of the oldest vessel in its VLCC fleet.

The shipowner said in a February announcement, as reported by TradeWinds, that its revised 2025 financial year forecast, ending in March, is likely to rise 14.3% to ¥14.4bn.

Higher-than-expected fourth-quarter equity-method income at Iino Kaiun likely reflects proceeds from the Kiho sale, providing an additional boost to the company’s bottom line.

Iino Kaiun’s sale of the Kiho leaves it with three VLCCs built between 2020 to 2021.

These are not likely to be up for sale given their young age, said shipbrokers.

VesselsValue estimated the Kiho’s market value at $53.52m, suggesting the vessel sold at a modest discount to brokers’ reported price of $51m.

It also comes as values for ageing VLCCs have shot up in recent weeks amid Sinokor’s shopping spree, with Clarksons estimating a 15-year-old VLCC at $72m in January, up from $59m at the start of December.

However, vessel age plays a strong role in determining resale values, said sources.

Market onlookers cited an equivalent VLCC sale — New York-listed tanker owner DHT Holdings’ 301,000-dwt DHT Bauhinia (built 2007) — for $51.5m at the end of January.

TradeWinds, using Clarksons Shipping Intelligence data, calculated that Sinokor’s shopping spree will give it a fleet of 55 VLCCs after the Kiho addition.
