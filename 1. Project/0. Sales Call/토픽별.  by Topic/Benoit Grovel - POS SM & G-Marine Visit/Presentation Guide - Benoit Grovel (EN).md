## Part 1 — LNG Market Overview: What Newbuilding Teams Need to Know

*(Source: BV LNG Carriers Presentation, Oct. 2025; Clarksons WFR Oct. 2025; IGU LNG World Report 2025)*

### The Market Duality: Short-Term Oversupply, Long-Term Growth

The current LNG carrier market presents a paradox that directly affects newbuilding decisions:

**Headwinds:**
- Current orderbook stands at ~325 vessels (48% of in-service fleet) with ~85 deliveries/year in 2026–2027
- Spot charter rates at ~$3,000/day vs. OPEX of ~$13,000/day
- Some new LNG terminal developments have been delayed
- USTR and trade war impact still under assessment

**Tailwinds:**
- LNG trade growth projected at 3.5–4% per year
- Fleet forecast to reach 1,049 vessels by 2030
- Large LNG terminal developments ongoing (US, Qatar, East Africa)
- Expected demand growth as LNG prices normalize
- New concepts reducing GHG emissions and OPEX

**Key message for newbuilding teams:** Vessels ordered today will enter service when the overcapacity cycle passes. Design choices made now will determine 20–25 years of OPEX competitiveness and regulatory compliance.

## Part 2 — Cargo Containment Systems (CCS): The Core Design Decision

*(BV LNG Carriers Presentation, Slides 15, 23–48)*

### Fleet & Orderbook Status (Clarksons, Oct. 2025)

| CCS Type | In-Service Fleet | Orderbook |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane (GTT) | 660 | 287 |
| Independent Type B (MOSS/SPB) | 111 | 0 |
| Independent Type A | 1 | 0 |

**Membrane is the only option being ordered for new vessels.** GTT (Gaztransport & Technigaz) dominates through two main technology families: NO (NO96 series) and MARK (Mark III series).

### The Three Latest GTT Technologies — All Approved by BV

**1. Mark III Flex+**
- Multiple vessels already delivered and in service
- Proven operational track record
- Dominant technology in current fleet

**2. NO96 Super+**
- First vessels delivered in early 2025
- Further improved BOR (Boil-Off Rate) vs. standard NO96
- Primary builder candidates: Hanwha Ocean, Hudong-Zhonghua

**3. GTT Next1 — BV Design Approval completed 2024**
- Hybrid architecture combining Mark III and NO96 principles
- Primary barrier: 1.2mm corrugated stainless steel (Mark III-style, 340mm spacing)
- Primary insulation: 230mm plywood/RPUF panels (1020×1020mm)
- Secondary barrier: 0.7mm Invar (NO96-style)
- Secondary insulation: 300mm plywood/RPUF (1020×1020mm)
- Total thickness: 530mm; RPUF density 130kg/m³ → lowest BOR of the three systems
- **No experience yet at Korean or Chinese yards** — a future newbuilding project by Pan Ocean or Glovis could be among the first applications

Hull structural requirements for Next1: inner hull elongation limited to 120 MPa (80 MPa dynamic); No.1 tank must be cylindrical; tank dimensions adapted for 340mm corrugation spacing.

### Innovative Multi-Tank Designs — All with BV AIP

| Design | BV AIP | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| GTT 174k — 3 tanks | 2022 | Remove 1 cofferdam & equipment → ↓ CAPEX/OPEX, ↓ BOR. Challenges: pump capacity, damage stability, sloshing |
| GTT 200k — 3 tanks | Sept. 2024 | BOR 0.06–0.075%, optimized for reduced operational speed. 3 tanks, ~300×48.9×26.9m |
| HD HHI 200k — 4 tanks | Sept. 2025 | 14 units for Dynagas under construction at HHI |
| QC-MAX 271k — 5 tanks | 2024 (AIP), Q3 2024 (1st contract) | Hanwha Ocean and Hudong designs; 24 vessels on order |

| GTT Trapezoidal No.1 tank | Issued | Increases capacity to 182,800 m³; Blue Amazon JDP (BV involved) |

## Part 3 — Propulsion Systems: The Most Critical Newbuilding Decision

*(BV LNG Carriers Presentation, Slides 16–17, 49–62)*

### Propulsion Orderbook (Clarksons, Oct. 2025)

| System | In-Service | Orderbook |
|---|---|---|
| 2-stroke Dual Fuel (DF) | 322 | 275 |
| Steam Turbine | 210 | 0 |
| 4-stroke DFDE | 192 | 12 |
| 2-stroke Diesel | 48 | 0 |

**2-stroke DF is the clear market standard.** Steam turbines are being retired (spot rates at $3,000/day vs. OPEX $13,000/day make them economically unviable).

### ME-GI (MAN) vs. X-DF (WinGD): The Key Decision

| Parameter | ME-GI (MAN) | X-DF (WinGD) |
|---|---|---|
| Injection method | High pressure | Low pressure |
| Methane slip | ~0.2 g/kWh (very low) | ~3 g/kWh (higher) |
| Fuel efficiency | Excellent in gas mode | Good flexibility |
| Latest development | ME-GI preferred over ME-GA | iCER + VCR (Variable Compression Ratio) reducing slip |

**CRITICAL NOTE:** In **October 2024, MAN B&W decided to stop production of ME-GA engines.** This fundamentally changes the market dynamic — owners must choose between ME-GI (high pressure, lower methane slip) or X-DF (low pressure with improving slip reduction technology).

### Methane Slip — The Emerging Regulatory Risk

Methane slip is unburned methane from engines caused by crevice volumes and incomplete combustion. While current IMO MARPOL regulations do not mandate methane slip measurement, **it may be incorporated into EEDI Phase 4 calculations as equivalent CO₂**. Given IMO's accelerating GHG strategy, vessels ordered today should be designed with this risk in mind.

Available mitigation technologies:
- **WinGD:** iCER (on-engine recirculation, compact), VCR, EGR
- **Both engines:** spark ignition variants, electronic control optimization, oxidation catalysts (Oxicat), reduced crevice volume designs

### Power Take-Off (PTO)

Over **100 newbuilding 174k LNGCs** on order are specified with PTO systems. The main engine SFOC (specific fuel oil consumption) of a 2-stroke DF is lower than a 4-stroke DF generator, making PTO the most direct fuel-saving option during navigation. With a clutch on the engine side, PTI (Power Take-In) for hybrid propulsion is also possible.

### Air Lubrication System (ALS)

ALS is now being proposed as an option in many newbuilding projects, though actual installed numbers remain limited. Key considerations:
- Efficiency is sea-state dependent (harsh conditions reduce effectiveness)
- Air compressors require electrical power → best combined with PTO for net benefit
- Fuel savings must be validated against CAPEX

**BV has specific CFD-based capability** to assess ALS efficiency gains in combination with hull optimization.

### BOG Management: Reliquefaction vs. Sub-Cooling

The goal is to minimize Gas Combustion Unit (GCU) operation. Main commercial systems:

| System | Type | Capacity | Refrigerant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Babcock ecoSMRT | Reliquefaction | 1.8–1.9 t/h | Mixed refrigerant |
| Air Liquide TBF | Sub-cooling | Various | — |
| Cryostar EcoCHILL | Sub-cooling | — | N₂/He, magnetic bearings |
| Wärtsilä Compact Reliq | Reliquefaction | Various | N₂ |
| Joule-Thomson (ME-GI assoc.) | Reliquefaction | — | — |

A **Full Reliquefaction** system that covers 100% of BOG is the emerging design trend for highest efficiency newbuilds.

### Hull Optimization

Design speed is historically specified at 19.0–19.5 knots, but actual operational profiles show ships spending most time at 14–17 knots. **BV has CFD-based hull optimization capability** delivering up to 5% cumulative fuel savings when optimizing for realistic operational speed profiles across multiple sea states.

## Part 4 — Regulatory Framework: Compliance That Affects Newbuilding Decisions Now

*(BV LNG Carriers Presentation, Slides 32–36, 54)*

### IGC Code Revision — In Force 1 July 2028

Key milestones: amendments finalized at CCC10 (September 2024) → approved at MSC 109 (December 2024) → adoption deferred to MSC 112 → **entry into force 1 July 2028**.

Relevant changes for newbuilding teams:

- **Chapter 6:** New metallic materials accepted for cryogenic applications (e.g., austenitic high-manganese steel) — enables new Type B tank designs
- **Chapter 15:** Updated filling limits
- **Chapter 16:** Other cargoes (e.g., ammonia) accepted as fuel
- **Chapter 17:** CO₂ as cargo — toxicity classification, non-flammable with some waivers

### IMO EEXI & CII

CII (Carbon Intensity Indicator) ratings for 2-stroke DF LNG carriers are projected to decline approximately **one rating grade every ~2 years** under the 3% annual reduction requirement. Vessels specified today need to be designed with long-term CII performance in mind.

### EU ETS & FuelEU Maritime (2026 onwards)

EU-calling vessels face carbon cost obligations from 2026 — potentially several million USD per vessel per year depending on routes. BV offers **CII prediction services** and **Energy Efficiency notations** to help owners quantify exposure during the design phase.

## Part 5 — Next-Generation Technologies: Designing for 2035 and Beyond

*(BV LNG Carriers Presentation, Slides 30, 63–65)*

### Onboard Carbon Capture (OCC)

While not yet counted by IMO regulations toward GHG reduction targets, OCC is advancing rapidly. BV's involvement includes:

- **SEAPEAK ARWA** (BV-classed): Carbon capture demonstration campaign, August 2023 – January 2024
- **SHI – Panasia System**: BV AiP covering safety of vessel and equipment (2023)
- **Rotoboost precombustion system**: Converts natural gas onboard into H₂-rich gas (89% hydrogen + unreacted NG) with solid carbon as byproduct; BV AiP issued 2023

The **OCC-PREPARED notation** allows a vessel to be designed and built with space reserved for future installation — a low-cost hedge against future carbon regulations.

### Wind-Assisted Ship Propulsion (WASP)

BV has completed a Joint Development Project with **HHI, Hyundai Global Service, MOL, and TotalEnergies** covering WASP integration on LNG carriers (and VLCCs) under BV NR206. Three technologies were evaluated:

- Rotor sail (Norsepower)
- Wing sail (AYRO)
- Wing sail (KSOE — relevant for Korean newbuilds)

AiP was issued. Key finding: number and location of wind propulsion systems is primarily governed by bridge visibility requirements.

### Type B Tanks — Revival Attempts

Korean and Chinese designers are proposing new Type B independent tank designs using high-manganese steel or aluminium. While membrane technology remains dominant, Type B tanks eliminate GTT licensing costs and are gaining renewed interest. New IGC Code Chapter 6 (from July 2028) explicitly recognizes austenitic high-Mn steel for cryogenic applications.

### Hybridization and Fuel Cells

Flexible power plant configurations combining 2-stroke DF main engines with energy storage systems (ESS), gas turbines, and fuel cells are under development. Still at varying TRL levels but relevant for 2030s newbuilding design discussions.

### Biofuels

Biofuels (bio-LNG, bio-methane) offer an **immediate, drop-in pathway for CII compliance** on existing and new dual-fuel LNG carriers without hardware changes. BV's **BIOFUEL READY notation** supports this strategy.

- Energy balance and fuel consumption

This is directly applicable to any new Korean-yard LNG carrier project.
