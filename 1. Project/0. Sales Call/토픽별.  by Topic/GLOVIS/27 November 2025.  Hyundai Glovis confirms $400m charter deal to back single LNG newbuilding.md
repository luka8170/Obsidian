
# Hyundai Glovis confirms $400m charter deal to back single LNG newbuilding

The company was previously known to have been in talks with yards

South Korean shipowner Hyundai Glovis has signed a long-term charter deal with Japanese trader Itochu that will back a single LNG carrier newbuilding.

Hyundai Glovis said it had inked a time charter covering a period of up to 15 years worth around KRW 580bn ($396.7m). But it did not name the counterparty, simply describing them as a global trading company.

The South Korean owner said it will build a 174,000-cbm LNG carrier for the contract, which is scheduled to be in operation shipping US cargoes from 2029.

Brokers told TradeWinds that Hyundai Glovis has opted to order the vessel with HD Hyundai group yards.

Earlier this month, this publication reported that Hyundai Glovis was in talks with both Hanwha Ocean and HD Hyundai Heavy Industries for up to two LNG carrier newbuilding berths.

In early October, TradeWinds revealed that the Korean shipping company had snared a long-term charter from Itochu in an off-market deal.

A charter rate in the high $70,000 to low-$80,000-per-day range was mentioned for the business.

Hyundai Glovis is traditionally known for its car carrier interests but has been diversifying into the energy sector.

The company built an LNG carrier for Australia’s Woodside Energy, and, in 2024, it teamed up with Japan’s K Line on four vessels contracted at Hanwha Ocean against charters with QatarEnergy.
