TO:                    IRSA PRINCIPALS

FROM:              IRSA SECRETARIAT – COMMERCIAL MANAGEMENT LTD.

 

 

As has been reported to members before, the MOT has launched an investigation into allegations by the China Shippers Association (CSA) that shipping lines in China are colluding and imposing unreasonable surcharges on China exports.  MOT has tasked the Shanghai Shipping Exchange (SSE) to carry out this investigation.  SSE requested a meeting with IRSA (and all other discussion agreements) as part of their research and investigation.  We delegated our legal counsel based in Beijing to represent us at this meeting, and we worked with him on how to respond to questions or allegations.  Attached is a report from our legal counsel following their meeting with SSE.

 

As reported by legal counsel, the meeting went quite smoothly, as SSE are only trying to collect information and understanding of our industry practices in China.  It was made clear from our legal counsel’s replies to SSE that many of the alleged miscellaneous surcharges CSA complain about are applied on an independent action basis individually by shipping lines, freight forwarders, or other middle-men, and have little/no relationship to IRSA as such.  Furthermore, all the local surcharges IRSA discuss and jointly agree (e.g. THC and doc fee) are published and filed with the MOT.  As shipping lines’ essential service to ocean freight payers is the transport of loaded goods on board their vessels, miscellaneous surcharges for services outside of this scope and beyond carriers’ control need to be recovered separately.  In this respect, shipping lines will follow the buying terms between the exporter/importer (where does responsibility for the cargo shift from the seller to the buyer) in determining which surcharges are collected from which party at origin/destination.

 

Attached is for your information only at this point in time.  SSE’s investigation is expected to run for the next two years.  We will, accordingly, continue to keep you informed on these developments.  In the interim, however, we would remind all members to exercise discretion in how you apply miscellaneous surcharges in China so that it is clear which are jointly agreed on a voluntary and non-binding basis by IRSA (i.e. only THC and doc fee) and which are applied on an independent action basis.  Furthermore, should you be called for clarification by SSE as part of their investigation, we would urge that you respond accurately based on IRSA filed action items.
