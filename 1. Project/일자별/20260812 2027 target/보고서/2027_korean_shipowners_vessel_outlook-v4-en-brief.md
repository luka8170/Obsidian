# 2027 Outlook for Korean Shipowners — Quick Summary

A short background summary to help set the 2027 sales target. All numbers come from Clarkson Research (World Fleet Monitor, July 2026) and related Clarkson reports — no outside news sources were used.

## The Three Key Numbers

| Item                | 2026 So Far                                                 | 2027 Outlook                                              |
| :------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| New ship orders     | 3.05M GT in H1 (67 ships)                                   | About the same, maybe a bit lower                         |
| Ship deliveries     | 2.7M GT full-year (1.6M already delivered + 1.1M still due) | 3.9M GT — about 44% more than 2026                        |
| Used ship purchases | 10.2M GT in H1 (70 ships) — world's No. 1 buyer             | Likely a sharp drop back toward the 2025 level (~3.2M GT) |

## Why

- **New orders will stay flat or dip slightly.** Shipyards are already booked for 3+ years and prices are high, so owners are in no rush to order more.
- **Deliveries will jump.** Ships ordered during the 2020–2022 boom are now finishing construction. This is a fixed schedule that's already locked in, not a forecast.
- **Used ship purchases should fall back.** The huge 2026 number was driven mostly by one giant one-off deal (Sinokor's VLCC purchase). Once that fades, volume should return closer to the 2025 level.

## What This Means for the Target

- **New orders and used ship buying: keep the target flat.** Don't assume growth — especially for used ships, where the 2026 surge is unlikely to repeat.
- **Deliveries are where the real growth is** — but most owners keep the same classification society they used when ordering, so more deliveries don't automatically mean more new class business. Still, it's worth building in a small increase for class maintenance and re-registration work.
