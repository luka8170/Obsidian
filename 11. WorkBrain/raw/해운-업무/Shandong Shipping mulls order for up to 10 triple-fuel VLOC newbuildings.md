The shipping company has inked MoU with favoured yard to build the Guaibamaxes

Shandong Marine Group, the parent company of Shandong Shipping, returns to Qingdao Beihai Heavy Industry.

Published 2 April 2026, 09:43

Chinese owner Shandong Shipping Corp is set to expand its growing fleet with triple-fuel large ore carriers.

A state-owned enterprise, Shandong Shipping is contracting one of its preferred shipyards — Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding Heavy Industry — to build a series of 325,000-dwt newbuildings.

The two companies, together with Chinese lessor Minsheng Financial Leasing, inked a memorandum of understanding for the triple-fuel ore carriers project during the 2026 Qingdao International Shipping Week.

Shandong Shipping did not disclose the number of vessels it plans to order nor their delivery dates.

Shipbuilding players put the number at 10, with construction expected to begin with two vessels.

A source close to Shandong Shipping said the project for the triple-fuel Guaibamaxes is still in the “making” and nothing is firm.

Shipbuilding players believe Shandong Shipping will be ordering the raft of newbuildings against long-term charters with Brazilian mining giant Vale.

In February, TradeWinds reported that Vale was seeking 30 ore carrier newbuildings — 10 ships of 325,000 dwt and 20 newcastlemaxes of 210,000 dwt — that can run on high-sulphur fuel oil, ethanol or methanol.

The mining company was reported to have floated tenders for time charters or contracts of affreightment for 25 years or more for the 30 vessels.

Besides being triple fuel, Vale is also requesting the new ships be fitted with scrubbers, rotor sails, air lubricant systems, shaft generators and other energy-saving devices.

A dry bulk market player said that Vale’s tender has attracted several Far Eastern shipping companies, including Shandong Shipping, but has seen limited participation from European firms.

Brokers suggest the triple-fuel Guaibamax would cost about $130m, against $115m to $118m for a conventionally fuelled vessel.

They believe the 325,000-dwt ore carriers will not be delivering soon as Qingdao Beihai is sitting on a large orderbook with berths booked up to 2030.

Shandong Shipping already has 10 methanol dual-fuel ships worth approximately $1.3bn being built at Qingdao Beihai. Delivery is set for between 2027 and 2029.

The company ordered them last year on the back of long-term contracts of affreightments with Vale.

Clarksons data shows the orderbook for Guaibamaxes stands at 32, including Shandong Shipping’s newbuildings at Qingdao Beihai.

Germany’s Neu Seeschiffahrt has six scrubber-fitted but methanol-ready 342,000-dwt ships at China Merchants Shipbuilding Industry Qingdao for delivery between 2029 and 2031, while Singapore’s Winning International Group has 16 vessels on order at two shipyards to be delivered from second half of this year and 2029.

Qingdao Beihai and Hengli Heavy Industry are each building eight vessels.
