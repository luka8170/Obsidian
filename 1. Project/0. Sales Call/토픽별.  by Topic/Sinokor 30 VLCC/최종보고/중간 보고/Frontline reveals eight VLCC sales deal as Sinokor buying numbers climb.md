
# Frontline reveals eight VLCC sales deal as Sinokor buying numbers climb

South Korean owner’s fleet set to number more than 100 owned and chartered VLCCs

John Fredriksen’s Frontline is selling eight of its oldest VLCCs to an unnamed owner for $831.5m as talk intensifies that Sinokor Maritime’s buying spree has climbed to more than 30 large tankers.

Frontline said on Thursday that it will deliver its eight first-generation eco VLCCs, built in 2015 and 2016, to a new owner in the first quarter of 2026.

The deal would price the ships at an average of more than $100m each.

After repayment of existing debt on the vessels, the transaction is expected to generate net cash proceeds of about $486m, with the expectation of a first-quarter gain in the range of $217.4m to $226.7m, depending on the delivery date of each vessel.

In parallel, Frontline is acquiring nine latest-generation scrubber-fitted eco VLCC newbuilding contracts for $1.224bn from an affiliate of Hemen Holding, its largest shareholder and Fredriksen’s private company.

Six of these vessels are under construction at Hengli Heavy Industry and three at Dalian Shipbuilding Industry Co in China.

Seven of the vessels are due for delivery from the third quarter of this year and the other two in the first and second quarters of 2027.

Frontline intends to finance this acquisition with cash and long-term debt financing.

Chief executive Lars Barstad said: “These two transactions enable Frontline to renew its fleet by replacing 10-year-old first-generation eco vessels with latest-generation, scrubber-fitted eco vessels at very firm pricing.

“The acquisition also supports our objective of increasing exposure to the VLCC segment without adding to overall vessel supply.

“The delivery schedule is particularly attractive, falling within a period that is generally considered closed to newbuild orders.”

Frontline’s announcement came at the end of a day packed with intense market talk about the extent of tanker-hungry Sinokor Maritime’s VLCC purchases.

During the past two days, Frontline’s name emerged as a seller of between two and 10 vessels to Sinokor, but is now believed to have sold these eight to the Ga-Hyun Chung-led owner.

George Procopiou’s Dynacom has been named as another seller. One source said the Greek owner could have sold up to 13 VLCCs to Chung’s company.

Sinokor is now understood to have acquired more than 30 vintage VLCCs in a Christmas and New Year holiday buying spree.

Those following the business described Sinokor as executing its purchases “stealthily”. Sellers generally did not know they were part of a broader campaign, at least until the deal was concluded, one source explained.

Sinokor has boosted its VLCC fleet further with a slew of fresh one to three-year time charters on tonnage, plus extensions on existing hires.

The company is listed on databases with an existing owned VLCC fleet of more than 20 vessels and is understood to have more than 40 ships on time charter. Its latest purchases are expected to bump up its VLCC fleet to 100-plus vessels.

Earlier, TradeWinds listed some of the VLCCs apparently sold to the South Korean owner.

They include vessels from CMB.Tech, which announced a six-ship VLCC sale to an unnamed owner on Wednesday; Capital Maritime; Cardiff Marine’s TMS Tankers; Advantage Tankers; and Kyklades Maritime.

A new name entered the picture on Thursday: New York-based International Seaways was said to have sold two VLCCs built in 2010 and 2012 to Sinokor — a past counterparty in tanker transactions.

Seaways chief executive Lois Zabrocky declined to comment when contacted by TradeWinds.

Sinokor has declined to comment on all of these reports.
