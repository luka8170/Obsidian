## Strategic Discussion: Wind Propulsion Technology

### Chairman Han's Vision
During the meeting, Chairman Han expressed significant interest in **wind-assisted propulsion technology** as a strategic direction for the group's future fleet development. This emerged as the primary focus area for potential BV collaboration.

### Technical Requirements Identified

#### Target Vessel Categories
Based on post-meeting email correspondence from GIGA Director Park Hwa-ok:

**Category A: Container Carriers (LOLO)**
- **Size Range**: 300-1,000 TEU
- **Application**: Feeder services and regional routes
- **Market Fit**: Aligns with GIGA's commercial shipping focus

**Category B: RoRo/Ropax Vessels**
- **Length**: 150-250m LOA
- **Beam**: 25-35m
- **Application**: Multi-modal transportation services

#### Performance Targets
- **Wind Power Contribution**: 40-90% of ship's propulsion power
- **Operating Conditions**: Pre-determined wind conditions and routes
- **Fuel Efficiency**: Significant reduction in conventional fuel consumption
- **Environmental Impact**: Substantial CO₂ emission reduction

### Market Context and Timing
Chairman Han's interest in wind propulsion aligns with global maritime decarbonization trends and recent technological developments:

#### Industry Development Examples
Based on Chairman Han's research interest in global wind propulsion projects:

**1. Commercial Success Stories**
- **Pyxis Ocean**: Bulk carrier with WindWings technology achieving 30% wind propulsion
- **Canopée**: Ariane 6 rocket transport vessel with OceanWings system
- **Neoliner Origin**: First commercial wind-powered RoRo vessel (delivery 2025)

**2. Container Ship Applications**
- **Windcoop Project**: 210 TEU container vessel with 1,050㎡ wing sails
- **CWS Airfin350**: Asymmetric rigid wing sail technology
- **Multiple French Projects**: Demonstrating commercial viability

**3. Performance Benchmarks**
- **Fuel Savings**: 20-90% depending on route and conditions
- **CO₂ Reduction**: 60-90% compared to conventional vessels
- **Economic Viability**: Positive ROI through reduced fuel costs

## Conclusion and Next Steps

### Meeting Outcomes

This strategic meeting with Polaris Shipping Chairman Han Hee-seung marks a significant shift in the business relationship from traditional classification services to innovative technology partnership. While the immediate container ship newbuilding projects have been suspended due to market conditions, the emergence of wind propulsion technology as a strategic focus creates substantial new opportunities for collaboration.

### Strategic Alignment

The convergence of Polaris Group's forward-thinking leadership, BV's global technical expertise, and the growing commercial viability of wind propulsion technology creates an ideal foundation for transformative partnership. Chairman Han's personal interest in wind technology, combined with GIGA's commercial shipping focus, provides the perfect testing ground for innovative green shipping solutions.

### Success Factors

#### **Technical Excellence**
- Leveraging BV's global network and expertise
- Accessing cutting-edge wind propulsion technologies
- Implementing rigorous testing and validation protocols
- Ensuring safety and regulatory compliance

#### **Commercial Viability**
- Focusing on economically attractive vessel segments
- Selecting high-wind-potential trading routes

- Building market demand for green shipping services

#### **Strategic Partnership**
- Aligning long-term interests and objectives
- Sharing risks and rewards appropriately
- Maintaining open communication and collaboration
- Adapting to market changes and opportunities

### Vision for the Future

By 2030, Polaris Shipping Group could emerge as the leading Korean shipping company in wind-assisted propulsion technology, operating a fleet of efficient, environmentally responsible vessels that combine economic competitiveness with environmental stewardship. This partnership with Bureau Veritas will be instrumental in achieving this vision, providing the technical expertise, global connections, and regulatory support necessary for successful implementation.

The journey from traditional fossil fuel propulsion to wind-assisted technology represents more than just an operational change – it embodies a fundamental transformation in how shipping companies can create value while protecting the environment. Through careful planning, systematic implementation, and unwavering commitment to excellence, this partnership can set new standards for sustainable shipping in Korea and beyond.
