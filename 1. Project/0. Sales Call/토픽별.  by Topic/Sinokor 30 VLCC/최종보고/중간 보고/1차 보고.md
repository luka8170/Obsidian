**3. The 2024 Lesson: Why We Lost 8 VLCCs to KR**

  

In 2024, Sinokor offered 8 new VLCCs to BV with one condition: "even a slight FYF reduction" as a symbolic partnership gesture. BV declined any flexibility citing profitability concerns. Result: All 8 vessels transferred to Korean Register (KR).

  

KR's winning formula: (1) Chairman-level visit demonstrating senior commitment, (2) Significant FYF reductions across Sinokor's entire fleet. Regretfully, BV's unwillingness to show any flexibility was interpreted by Sinokor owner’s son not as financial prudence but as indifference to the relationship.

  

Current situation (January 10, 2026): Sinokor repeats the identical message – "if BV shows pricing flexibility, we'll seriously consider BV classification." The barrier remains, but the opportunity is now 4x larger.

  

  

**Key Insight**: Sinokor's owner's son (who makes the final classification decision) is asking "Will BV fight for our business?" not "What is your exact price?” 8 VLCC CAC to KR in 2024  wasn't about price level—it was about showing zero flexibility. Our 2024 response significantly hardened Sinokor's stance toward BV, creating a negative impact on the relationship.
