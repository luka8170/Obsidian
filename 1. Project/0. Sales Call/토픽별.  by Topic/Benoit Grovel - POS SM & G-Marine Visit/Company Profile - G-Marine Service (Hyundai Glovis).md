Hyundai Glovis was established in 2001 as the **integrated logistics arm of Hyundai Motor Group**, primarily to handle inbound parts logistics and outbound vehicle distribution for Hyundai Motor and Kia. It has since grown into South Korea's leading logistics company, with a fleet of **92+ PCTCs (Pure Car and Truck Carriers)** — one of the world's largest PCTC operators.

The company is now executing a major strategic transformation: diversifying from an automotive-captive logistics provider into a **global multi-cargo shipping company**, with LNG and gas carriers as a core growth pillar.

## 2. G-Marine Service — The Ship Management Arm

**G-Marine Service** is Hyundai Glovis's dedicated **ship technical management** subsidiary. Its role is to handle the technical operations, crewing, and maintenance of Glovis's growing owned fleet — covering both the existing PCTC fleet and the newer LNG/gas carrier assets.

G-Marine Service has emerged as a key technical partner in Glovis's new vessel development programs, particularly in the area of **alternative fuels and next-generation vessel design**. In 2024, G-Marine Service co-developed the **K-Ammonia PCTC** design alongside HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Hyundai Glovis, receiving an Approval in Principle (AIP) from Korean Register (KR) at Posidonia 2024.

> **For Benoit:** G-Marine Service is the operational counterpart at the technical level. The newbuilding team will include G-Marine engineers who are directly responsible for specifying and overseeing new vessels — making them the key audience for BV's technical content.

## 4. Gas & LNG Strategy — Entering as a New Player with Big Ambitions

### The Strategic Logic

Hyundai Glovis's entry into gas shipping follows a clear logic:

1. **Captive demand from Hyundai Motor Group**: The group is a large industrial LNG consumer for factory energy and plans significant ammonia/hydrogen imports for future energy needs
2. **Revenue diversification**: Reduce dependency on automotive logistics (which faces EV transition disruption)
3. **Leverage Korean shipyard relationships**: Deep ties with HD HHI, Hyundai Samho, Samsung HI provide newbuilding access
4. **Energy transition positioning**: Ammonia, hydrogen, and CO₂ transport will be high-growth segments

### 2030 Investment Plan

Hyundai Glovis has announced a total investment of **KRW 9 trillion (~USD 6.5 billion)** through 2030, targeting:
- Revenue growth to **>KRW 40 trillion** (from KRW 28.4T in 2024)
- Operating profit margin of **7%**
- Fleet expansion across PCTCs, LNG carriers, and gas/chemical carriers

Gas shipping (LNG + ammonia + future fuels) is explicitly one of the **three strategic pillars** alongside PCTCs and logistics.

## 5. Technology & Innovation Initiatives

### K-Ammonia PCTC (G-Marine Service + HD HHI + Hyundai Glovis)

At **Posidonia 2024**, G-Marine Service, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, and Hyundai Glovis jointly presented a next-generation **K-Ammonia PCTC** — a pure car and truck carrier powered by ammonia fuel — and received an **AIP from Korean Register (KR)**. This is a direct indicator that G-Marine Service's engineering team is actively working at the frontier of alternative fuel vessel design.

> **For Benoit:** BV's equivalent work with Hanwha Ocean + Naftomar on ammonia-fueled VLAC (AiP, Gastech 2025) is a directly comparable achievement — this should be referenced in the conversation to demonstrate BV's parallel expertise.

### AI-Driven Autonomous Navigation

In July 2025, Hyundai Glovis announced the deployment of **AI-based autonomous navigation systems** on 7 PCTCs by end of H1 2026 — the first shipping company to deploy such systems on car carriers at scale. This signals a strong appetite for digital and technological innovation across the fleet, including future LNG and gas vessels.

### LNG Dual-Fuel PCTCs

Glovis plans to have **28 LNG-powered PCTCs** in operation. This extensive operational experience with LNG as fuel gives the newbuilding team direct familiarity with fuel supply systems, BOG management on a smaller scale, and crew training — highly relevant background for their LNG carrier ambitions.

## 7. Competitive Context & Key Challenges

### What Sets Glovis Apart (Strengths)

- Strong group-level financial backing (Hyundai Motor Group balance sheet)
- Deep Korean shipyard relationships → competitive newbuilding terms and early access to slots
- Practical experience with LNG as fuel (via PCTC fleet) building toward LNG carrier competency
- Active positioning in ammonia/hydrogen transport (future-focused)
- Willingness to pioneer new vessel types (K-Ammonia PCTC, first AI car carriers)

### Key Challenges for a New LNG Operator

- **Limited LNG carrier operational experience**: Only 1 vessel delivered as of 2025; technical management is still being built
- **LNG carrier market volatility**: Entering a sector with significant near-term overcapacity
- **Regulatory complexity**: New entrant must navigate IGC Code, SOLAS, MARPOL, CII, EU ETS simultaneously
- **Crewing and competency**: LNG carrier crew training and certification is specialized; G-Marine must build this pipeline
- **Ammonia safety for cargo**: VLGC ammonia operations require different safety protocols from LPG — learning curve is steep

> **For Benoit:** These challenges are precisely where BV adds value. Glovis is **a prime candidate for BV's early technical engagement (ETE) model** — involving BV from the very beginning of new vessel design to avoid costly rework and to navigate regulatory complexity efficiently.
