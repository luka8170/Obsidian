## FuelEU Maritime: The first-year verdict on pooling

## FuelEU Maritime’s first reporting year confirmed pooling as a practical, cost-efficient compliance tool. But success depended on early action, trusted partners and operational expertise.

#### Photo: Gasum.

The first reporting year under the FuelEU Maritime regulation is complete. Shipowners have submitted their compliance reports to EMSA and attention is already turning toward the next compliance cycle. For the maritime industry, 2025 was also the first real-world test of pooling. The results were clear: pooling works.

The year also showed pooling to be the preferred compliance mechanism across the industry. The regulation applies to all vessels above 5,000 gross tonnage calling at European ports, regardless of flag. Estimates suggest that more than 90% of vessels with compliance deficits opted for pooling solutions, reflecting both strong confidence in the model’s effectiveness and an existing need for a flexible solution.

For Gasum, the first year demonstrated both the strength of its pooling model and the growing maturity of the market.

“The first year proved we have the operational capacity and expertise required. There are always lessons to learn in a new regulatory environment, but overall, the year was highly successful,” says **Dabbert Malte**, Pooling Manager at Gasum.

Gasum’s pooling service combines vessels generating compliance surplus with vessels facing compliance deficits, creating commercial pools that allow shipping companies to meet the FuelEU Maritime requirements efficiently.

The first year also highlighted that successful pooling requires more than simply joining a pool. For many shipowners, navigating the reporting process proved more demanding than anticipated.

“The THETIS-MRV reporting system is not always straightforward to use. A single mistake can impact an entire pool structure,” Malte explains. “In addition to providing pooling services, our experts supported customers throughout the reporting process. Our approach has always been to offer holistic support, which naturally extends to FuelEU compliance as well.”

Gasum’s long experience in energy market services and regulatory compliance in other sectors provided an important advantage during a year when many surrounding systems and processes were still evolving.

Another clear takeaway from the first year was the importance of timing. Shipowners that secured their pooling strategy early benefited from smoother onboarding, greater flexibility and reduced administrative pressure. Those entering the market later faced tighter timelines, increasing complexity and potentially higher compliance costs.

Market developments indicate that demand may push prices upward closer to future compliance deadlines. While pricing for 2026 remains relatively moderate, capacity constraints are expected to tighten as available pools approach their limits.

The first year also demonstrated that transparency and trust matter. Not all pooling solutions offer the same level of traceability or operational security. Gasum manages the entire pooling value chain and can guarantee compliance unit availability through its own bio-LNG value chain. This provides customers with transparency regarding both the origin and reliability of compliance units. As a Finnish state-owned company, Gasum also offers additional assurance regarding governance and compliance standards.

“Customers want transparency and reliability, not just compliance units on paper. Early planning allows us to work together with customers to build the best possible solution and reduce uncertainty throughout the process,” says Malte.

Looking ahead, the pressure on shipping companies will only increase. FuelEU Maritime currently requires a 2% reduction in well-to-wake greenhouse gas intensity, rising to 6% in 2030 and continuing progressively toward an 80% reduction target by 2050.

For shipowners, the lessons from year one are already clear: pooling is a proven compliance tool, but preparation matters. Securing a reliable long-term partner early can reduce both costs and operational complexity as the regulation tightens in the years ahead.
