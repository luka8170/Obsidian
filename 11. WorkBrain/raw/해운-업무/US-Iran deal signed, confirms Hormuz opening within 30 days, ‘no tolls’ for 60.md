Interim peace deal fudges the question of tolls and grants waivers to Tehran to sell its oil until a final accord

![Dire straits. The Hormuz water way in a satellite picture.](https://image.dngroup.com/global/nhst/binary/a3ee7d1ce081e89e06054eeb7cc37905?crop=2390%2C1594%2Cx4%2Cy0%2Csafe&width=980&format=auto&quality=80)

Dire straits. The Hormuz water way in a satellite picture. Photo: Nasa

US and Iran remotely signed an interim peace deal late on Wednesday, which obliges the two rivals to gradually lift their current blockades in the Strait of Hormuz and the Arabian Sea within 30 days and allows Iran to sell its oil until a final agreement.

“The text of the Iran-US memorandum has now been officially finalised as both parties have signed it,” Iran's Tasnim news agency cited foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei as saying.

A formal signature ceremony originally planned to take place in Switzerland on 19 June is therefore no longer envisaged, Baqaei added.

The 14-point document also sets out that Iran will not charge any tolls for Hormuz transits during the coming 60 days, in which the two sides will keep bargaining to reach a comprehensive, final deal that includes Tehran’s nuclear programme.

The text, however, may be paving the way for Tehran to charge transiting ships in the future, as it recognises the right of Iran and its neighbor and ally Oman “to define the future administration and maritime services in the Strait of Hormuz in discussion with other Persian Gulf littoral states”.

This clause is in line with Iran’s position that the Strait of Hormuz is in sovereign Iranian and Omani waters and will be jointly run between the two countries under a future set of arrangements that should include tolls.

The charges may not be called tolls but instead fees for various maritime services, which the Islamic Republic claims to have been so far providing for free.

Baqaei reiterated early on Thursday that Tehran intends to collect fees for navigation, environmental protection, insurance and other maritime services,

The international shipping community has so far rejected any kind of charges for Hormuz transits.

Its concerns are reflected in a separate clause in the 14-point plan, which states that any Iranian-Omani management of Hormuz must be in line with “applicable international law” as well as with “the sovereign rights of coastal states”.

## Hormuz and US blockades to be lifted in lockstep

TradeWinds already reported on Monday, when Iran and the US first announced reaching the interim peace deal without disclosing its details, that the re-opening of the Strait to pre-war traffic levels would take place gradually within 30 days of the text entering into effect.

The 14-point text confirms this information, citing as a reason for the delay “the need for the removal of technical obstacles and the neutralisation of mines”.

The 30-day transition also applies to the US, which is supposed to gradually lift its maritime blockade of Iran’s seaborne trade over this period at the same pace in which Iran re-opens Hormuz.

The US blockade is already softening, as TradeWinds reported early on Wednesday, with Iran sending out its first laden VLCCs and suezmaxes since the US Navy started imposing it in April.

The US blockade has been porous, as the US Navy could not prevent every ship from sneaking into Iran, carrying goods into the Islamic Republic or adding to its floating oil storage capacity.

The measure, however, has been very successful in choking off Iran’s crude exports, which are the lifeline of the country’s economy.

Both Iran's and the US blockade have been deadly for seafarers.

Eleven seamen died as a result of Iranian attacks on oceangoing ships since the war broke out on 28 February.

US strikes on vessels violating Washington's blockade have killed another three seafarers. A fourth has died of medical neglect on board a vessel stranded for weeks in the Sea of Oman after the US Navy diverted it.

The gradual re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz will allow the IMO to push ahead with an evacuation plan to get hundreds of vessels still stranded in the Middle East Gulf.

## Sanctions relief in sight

In another development with effect on shipping and energy markets, the US pledged in the interim peace deal to immediately ”issue waivers for the export of Iranian crude oil, petroleum products and derivatives, and all associated services, including banking transactions, insurances, transportation, etc”.

The US even promised to lift all primary and secondary sanctions against Iran if a final peace agreement can be reached on the Islamic Republic's nuclear programme.
