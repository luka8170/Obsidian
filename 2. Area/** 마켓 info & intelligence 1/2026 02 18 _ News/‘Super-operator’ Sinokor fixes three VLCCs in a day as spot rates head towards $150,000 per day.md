Ambitious tanker owner Sinokor Maritime has been flexing its muscles in the VLCC spot market as rates keep pushing upwards.

Vessels controlled by the South Korean company, believed to be working together with MSC Mediterranean Shipping Co supremo Gianluigi Aponte on a series of VLCC purchases, accounted for three of the four big tanker fixtures reported on subjects on Tuesday by UK pools company Tankers International.

The 300,000-dwt DHT Osprey (built 2016) was fixed by the Ineos and PetroChina joint venture Petroineos at $85,000 per day.

Sinokor was earlier reported to have fixed the DHT Holdings ship on a three-year time charter at $52,500 per day.

The 320,000-dwt VL Prosperity (built 2015) was booked by TotalEnergies’ chartering unit CSSA at $95,000 per day, and the 297,000-dwt Plata West (built 2010) was taken by the Omani government’s OQ Trading at $97,000 per day.

The exception to Sinokor’s dominance was the Onassis-controlled 317,000-dwt Olympic Light (built 2011), fixed by Sinochem Oil Shipping in Singapore at $124,000 per day.

Tankers International rated all these deals as sharply up on Monday’s levels.

The Baltic Exchange assessed VLCC spot rates at $143,300 per day for modern scrubber-fitted ships fixing out of the Middle East Gulf to Asia on Tuesday, up 5% in a day and representing five-year highs.

BRS Shipbrokers said Sinokor is viewed as now controlling 16% of the compliant VLCC fleet, “taking the market by surprise”.

“There has never before been a single VLCC operator with such a dominant share of the active fleet,” it said.

On Monday, trader and owner Vitol paid $127,000 for a Kyklades Maritime VLCC and Frontline’s 303,000-dwt Front Empire (built 2018) was booked by Petrobras in Brazil at $127,000.

The highest headline rate came from the Mecuria-controlled 299,000-dwt Hercules I (built 2017), reported at $153,000 per day to Koch.

SB1 Markets analyst Oda Hanes Lund said the VLCC upcycle still has room to run due to “extremely tight” tonnage supply.

The analyst noted rising sanctions pressure and increased compliant seaborne volumes driving higher demand for mainstream ships, with Chinese and Indian imports of compliant crude up about 20% year on year.

Lund also said there was “constrained” compliant fleet growth, driven by a material share of the global fleet being sanctioned, alongside a rapidly ageing fleet.

This means effective fleet growth is still negative, tightening tanker markets and supporting higher charter rates and asset values.

“At the same time, charterers are increasingly seeking reliable tonnage amid constrained fleet availability, pushing time charter rates higher,” the analyst added.

One-year fixes have been pushed up to $90,000 per day as a result.

Current fixtures are rumoured at around $100,000 per day, with deals said to be concluded within a day compared to weeks in a softer market, SB1 believes.

The investment bank also said asset values have jumped as much as 30% in recent months, with both this and charter trends expected to continue.

Allied QuantumSea said spot rate ideas east of Suez were being shaped by how quickly early cargoes cleared against available tonnage.

In the Atlantic, West Africa to China runs were assessed at $106,500 per day, with owners focusing on workable prompt positions.

## Under the radar

UK broker Gibson said some activity had taken place under the radar, with industry players in London last week for International Energy Week.

Attention is moving to March loading dates, while enquiries picked up towards the end of the week, helping to stabilise the market.

John Fredriksen-controlled shipping company SFL Corp’s chief executive, Ole Hjertaker, told an earnings call: “There is a group of people working together who effectively control about one-third of the available or traded VLCC tanker fleet.”

He did not name names.
