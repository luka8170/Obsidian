Sinokor Maritime appears to have beefed up its VLCC fleet just before valuations began skyrocketing.

VesselsValue has the 320,000-dwt Portugal Prosperity (built 2011) being purchased by the South Korean tanker giant at an undisclosed price from Marshall Islands-registered Celest Marine.

The purchase would expand Sinokor’s position as the world’s largest owner of oil tankers.

The sale was agreed in June, when the vessel was worth approximately $78.2m.

Since then, following Adnoc Logistics & Services’ sweep for six ships at premium prices earlier this month, the ship is valued at $94m.

Meanwhile, the valuation service has 15-year-old VLCC values rising by 20% to just under $101m since the purported sale.

MSI Horizon values the ship between $72.9m and $85.2m and has five-year-old VLCC values climbing from $130m in the first quarter to $152m in the third quarter.

Until 1 August, the vessel was known as the Hippolyta and was flagged in the Marshall Islands, according to Equasis.

Since then, it has taken on its current name and was reflagged in Liberia.

The database lists its owner and commercial manager as Lindvallen Prosperity and its technical manager as Synergy Maritime Indonesia, with Lindvallen Prosperity taking over on 12 August and Synergy Maritime on 14 August.

Sinokor and partner Gianluigi Aponte of MSC Mediterranean Shipping Company spent the winter buying up dozens of VLCCs from sellers like Frontline, CMB.Tech, International Seaways and Zodiac Maritime.

The shopping spree saw Sinokor amass the world’s largest VLCC fleet with 93 ships, according to Clarksons, leapfrogging state-linked China Merchants Energy Shipping and Cosco Shipping with 51 and 41 VLCCs, respectively.

The South Korean company has kept buying beyond the usual winter months, including a July deal for the 318,400-dwt Algeria Prosperity (ex-Stephanie, built 2012).

At the time, market prices would have seen its Greek sellers, the Hadjiyannis family, [roughly double their money](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/greek-owner-doubles-money-on-vlcc-asset-play-to-sinokor-and-msc/2-1-2021537) on a $41.5m purchase price.
