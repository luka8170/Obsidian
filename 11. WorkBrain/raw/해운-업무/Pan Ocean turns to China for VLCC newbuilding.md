The diversified shipping company is making a big push in the VLCC sector

![Pan Ocean president and chief executive Ahn Joong Ho.](https://image.dngroup.com/global/nhst/binary/54fa320caff4cf26184058c7c5655be4?crop=1560%2C1040%2Cx344%2Cy0%2Csafe&width=1300&format=auto&quality=80)

Pan Ocean is expanding its VLCC fleet with an order for a newbuilding at a Chinese shipyard.

The order, valued at close to $122m, adds to a recent $676m purchase of 10 secondhand VLCCs by the Seoul-based company.

Pan Ocean disclosed the contract on Monday without naming the shipyard.

However, shipbuilding players point to CSSC-controlled Qingdao Beihai Heavy Industries as the yard contracted to build the 319,000-dwt crude carrier.

The South Korean shipping company said the VLCC, with delivery in 2029, will cost KRW 183.5bn, or approximately $122m based on the exchange rate it cited.

Pan Ocean said the VLCC is ammonia-ready, in line with the company’s decarbonisation strategy.

“The final decision will depend on market conditions for alternative fuels,” the company said.

Besides the single VLCC on order at Qingdao Beihai, Pan Ocean also has two additional VLCCs being built at HD Hyundai Samho Heavy Industries.

The company ordered the pair of conventionally fuelled 300,000-dwt tankers in 2025 for a reported price of around $127m each.

They are slated for delivery in June and August 2027.

Pan Ocean was reported to have ordered the pair to strengthen its position in the liquid bulk market.

Clarksons’ data shows Pan Ocean currently owns two VLCCs – the DSME-built 300,000-dwt Grand Ambition and Grand Bonanza (both built 2021).

In February, the company expanded its large tanker footprint significantly by paying $676m on 10 secondhand VLCCs from compatriot SK Shipping.

The latest VLCC order is Pan Ocean’s second newbuilding contract this year.

In February, the company ordered up to four newcastlemax bulk carriers at the same yard for an average of $77m apiece — two firm vessels plus two options, the latter of which it has since exercised.

The 210,000-dwt ships, due for delivery between the second half of 2030 and the first quarter of 2031, will be equipped with dual-fuel propulsion capable of running on LNG or ammonia.

Clarksons data shows Pan Ocean also has five MR tankers and four ultramax bulk carrier newbuildings on order at two other shipyards.

HD Hyundai Mipo Vietnam is constructing the 50,000-dwt product carriers, with delivery from this year and 2027.

Oshima Shipbuilding is building the series of 64,000-dwt bulk carriers, with two vessels due for delivery this year and one each in 2027 and 2028.

Pan Ocean operates a fleet of 115 vessels spanning VLOCs, LNG carriers, MR tankers and bulk carriers, with 12 vessels currently under construction in Japan and South Korea, including two VLCCs and five MR tankers.

The company is the shipping arm of Harim, South Korea’s leading poultry processor.
