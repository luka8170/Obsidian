Four LNG carriers sold this year by Oman’s Asyad Shipping have been sanctioned by the UK after they re-emerged under the Russian flag.

The quartet is currently shipping cargoes supplied by Russia’s sanctioned Arctic LNG 2 plant to East Asia.

The LNG carriers are among 27 ships designated by the UK on Tuesday in its latest sanctions package timed for release as G7 leaders meet in France.

The package also targets Russian shipping insurers and Murmansk-based Kord-Bunker, part of Arctic tanker specialist Kordoil.

UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer said: **“** These sanctions target the vessels, the money and the actors propping up Russia’s war economy, and in turn, threatening European security.”

Asyad confirmed its en-bloc sale of the four LNG carriers in February. The sale price was about $110m for the four tankers, all more than 20 years old.

After several rapid name and flag changes, they emerged as the 147,684-cbm Orion and 148,174-cbm Kosmos (both built 2005), the 147,100-cbm Merkuriy and 147,384-cbm Luch (both built 2006).

The remaining vessels on the new UK list are tankers linked to the Russian oil trade, including some that have been sold by European owners in the past 12 months.

They include the 159,100-dwt Elbus (built 2005), which was attacked by Ukrainian drones in the Black Sea less than three months after it was sold by Greek shipowner Eurotankers.

The UK sanctions list also includes the 105,600-dwt Atmos (built 2007), which was sold in September 2025 by Greek shipowner Polembros Shipping for $28m, according to VesselsValue.

There is no suggestion that Polembros or the other former owners knew the vessels would be sold into the shadow fleet.

“Those who are suspected of enabling the sale of tankers to Russia’s shadow fleet will be exposed and face action,” the UK’s Foreign Office said.

The sanctions came as G7 and EU leaders met at Evian-les-Bains, joined by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The Ukrainian leader met US President Donald Trump during the meeting.

The latest sanctions came as the captain of the first Russian shadow fleet tanker seized by the UK was due to appear in court in Southampton, England.

Indian national Ajay Pant, 38, the master of the 106,800-dwt Smyrtos (built 2009), has been accused of breaching sanctions by delivering Russian oil. The British military boarded the ship in the English Channel on Sunday.

The European Union also unveiled a new sanctions package this week that targets a series of ship managers and operators, an insurance broker and a trader.
