1. Sinokor Merchant Marine (S&P Strategy)  
Headline: Sinokor Eyes Suezmaxes as VLCC Prices Overheat  
Detailed Analysis: According to market sources in London and Athens, Sinokor Merchant Marine is reportedly inspecting 3 to 4 Suezmax tankers (approx. 150,000 DWT, built circa 2016).This move is interpreted as a "Gap Filling" strategy. Over the last month, Sinokor’s aggressive acquisition of over 30 VLCCs drove benchmark prices up by nearly 20%. Consequently, the price gap between VLCCs and Suezmaxes has widened beyond historical averages. Sinokor appears to be shifting focus to Suezmaxes, which are currently viewed as undervalued relative to the overheated VLCC sector.  
Market Implication: Brokers warn that this could trigger a "second wave" of asset inflation. Sellers in the Suezmax market are already withdrawing offers, anticipating that Sinokor’s "vacuum-style" buying will reset price benchmarks just as it did for VLCCs.

Multiple shipbuilding players said the liner giant has turned to the shipbuilding market to secure its own tonnage.

TradeWinds has learned that MSC is in serious discussions with China’s fast-expanding Hengli Heavy Industry, also known as Hengli Shipbuilding, for up to 10 newbuildings.

The company is understood to be looking at an order for six firm ships plus a further four optional slots.

One tanker player, who said he had heard of MSC’s interest in the VLCC newbuildings, said: “There were strong rumors that they are negotiating for some VLCCs with deliveries scheduled between late 2028 and 2029.”

Those following the business said MSC will fully own the VLCCs. But there is widespread speculation that the vessels would be chartered out, possibly to the company’s apparent new tanker partner, Sinokor.

TradeWinds has contacted MSC and Sinokor for comment.

Tanker newbuildings would be a new departure for MSC from its traditional diet of container ships.
