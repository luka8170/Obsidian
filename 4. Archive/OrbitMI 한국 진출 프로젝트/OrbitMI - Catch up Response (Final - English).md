# OrbitMI - Catch up Response (Final Version - English)

**Subject: RE: OrbitMI - Catch up**

Dear Jack,

Thank you for your email and continued interest in the Korean market.

I would like to share some important market insights that I believe will be helpful for your strategic planning regarding the questions you raised.

**SK Shipping & Hyundai Glovis - Market Reality**

Following further investigation and discussions with both SK Shipping and Hyundai Glovis, I need to share some critical information:

Both companies are currently **officially utilizing Vessel Link services (https://home.vessellink.com/)**. The inquiry from SK Shipping that you mentioned was confirmed to be purely for **market survey purposes** - they were gathering competitive intelligence on alternative solutions in the market while maintaining their existing Vessel Link contract. There is no active evaluation or procurement process underway.

Notably, Hyundai Glovis officially entered into a strategic partnership with Lab021 for Vessellink deployment in July 2025, which was formally announced via Business Wire (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250716141805/en/Hyundai-Glovis-Partners-with-Lab021-to-Deploy-Vessellink). Therefore, any new opportunities with Hyundai Glovis appear to be effectively unavailable.

**Korean Market Landscape and Vessel Link's Market Dominance**

I feel it's important to be transparent about the current market dynamics:

1. **Vessel Link's Market Dominance**: Vessel Link (operated by Lab021) currently holds a commanding position in the Korean shipowner market. Most major Korean shipowners are either actively using their services or in advanced stages of contract execution. Lab021 already provides the Vessellink platform to more than 700 vessels and is further strengthening its market dominance among Korean shipowners and shipyards through partnerships with companies like Accelleron (https://cyprusshippingnews.com/2025/10/30/accelleron-extends-reach-of-loreka360-digital-solutions-in-korea-with-lab021-tie-up/).

2. **Recent Market Activities**: Lab021 is conducting aggressive marketing activities to penetrate the Korean market. They regularly host customer invitation events, technical seminars, and workshops for Korean shipowners, building close relationships. Additionally, they are solidifying their market position by providing integrated solutions to Korean shipowners through strategic alliances with global technology partners.

3. **Local Presence Requirement**: Korean shipowners consistently emphasize the critical importance of having **local organization, dedicated Korean staff, and proximity for training and after-sales service**. This is often a non-negotiable requirement in their vendor selection criteria. Lab021 has its headquarters in Busan with a Korean-speaking local organization, providing direct communication and immediate technical support to Korean shipowners. This local capability represents a significant entry barrier that overseas-based solution providers find difficult to overcome in the short term.

4. **Market Entry Barriers**: Given the above factors, while one-time visits to Korea for relationship building may have value, tangible commercial results in the near term are unlikely without establishing solid local infrastructure. Lab021 continues to strengthen its position in the Korean market through regular customer events, a Busan-based local organization, and alliances with major global partners.

**Regarding the 2026 Visit**

While I always appreciate the opportunity to meet in person, careful consideration of the timing and expected ROI for a Korea visit in early 2026 appears warranted. Considering the current market conditions and competitive landscape:

- Vessel Link has already secured long-term contracts with major shipowners (including Hyundai Glovis)
- Continuous customer relationship reinforcement through regular customer events and technical seminars
- Extremely difficult structure for new entrants to displace existing relationships without local organization
- Conservative approach of Korean shipowners toward vendor changes

Given these factors, the Korean market would require substantial long-term investment and a strategic approach (establishing local entity, building Korean-language support organization, hosting regular customer events, etc.) rather than periodic visits.

**Moving Forward**

I wanted to provide you with this honest assessment so you can make informed decisions about resource allocation and market entry strategy. Should there be any significant changes in the market landscape or if specific opportunities arise that warrant OrbitMI's involvement, I will certainly keep you informed.

In the meantime, I wish you and the OrbitMI team continued success in your other markets.

Best regards,
