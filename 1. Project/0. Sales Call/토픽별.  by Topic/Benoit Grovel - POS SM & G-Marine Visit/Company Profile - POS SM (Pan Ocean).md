Pan Ocean was established in 1966 and has grown into one of South Korea's leading shipping companies, with a worldwide reputation built primarily in the **dry bulk carrier** segment. Since 2013, the company has been part of **Harim Group**, a major Korean food and agribusiness conglomerate whose grain import requirements give Pan Ocean a strategically captive cargo base. Pan Ocean is also pursuing agri-trading as a new business pillar, integrating its shipping expertise with the parent group's supply chain.

## 3. LNG & Gas Business — The Fast-Growing Segment

### Fleet Development Timeline

Pan Ocean entered LNG shipping in **December 2008** through the **KOGAS (Korea Gas Corporation) LNG Import Project**, operating a 153,000 cbm LNG carrier as part of Korea's national energy security infrastructure. This long-term charter remains the anchor of Pan Ocean's gas business.

Since 2020, Pan Ocean has aggressively expanded through long-term charter agreements with global oil majors:

| Charterer | Vessel Size | Charter Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| KOGAS | 153,000 cbm | Long-term | Since 2008 — anchor contract |
| Shell | 174,000 cbm × 2 | Long-term | Part of Shell global LNG fleet strategy |
| Galp (Portugal) | 174,000 cbm | Long-term | *New Apex* — delivered from SHI |
| QatarEnergy | 174,000 cbm | Long-term | Korean consortium; BV class |
| Shell | 18,000 cbm LNGBV | Long-term | LNG bunkering vessel, deployed in Americas |

### LNG Fleet Target: 13 Vessels by End 2025

Pan Ocean set a target to operate **13 LNG-related vessels** (including LNG bunkering vessels) by end of 2025. This represents a ~10-fold expansion from a single vessel in 2008.

### Newbuilding Investment: KRW ~1.6 Trillion

Pan Ocean has publicly announced approximately **KRW 1.6 trillion (≈ USD 1.2 billion)** of committed investment in LNG and gas carrier newbuildings, making it one of the most aggressive Korean LNG shipping investors relative to its size.

**Key newbuilding characteristics:**
- Standard size: **174,000 cbm** (dominant choice for commercial flexibility — can call at most global LNG terminals)
- Containment system: **Mark III Flex membrane** (GTT) — confirmed for multiple units
- Propulsion: **MAN Energy Solutions** engines specified for several units — notably early orders included MAN ME-GA (low-pressure 2-stroke DF), though post-October 2024 MAN ME-GA discontinuation, future orders may shift to ME-GI or WinGD X-DF
- Shipyard: Primarily **Samsung Heavy Industries (SHI)** and **Hanwha Ocean**

### LNG Bunkering Vessels

Pan Ocean and Shell have developed a partnership in LNG bunkering, with Pan Ocean owning and operating **18,000 cbm LNG bunkering vessels** chartered by Shell for deployment in the Americas. This demonstrates Pan Ocean's capability across the LNG value chain — not just large-scale transport.

## 5. Strategic Direction & Future Outlook

### Near-Term (2025–2027)
- Delivery of remaining newbuild LNG carriers on order
- Full deployment of LNG bunkering fleet under Shell charter
- Continued OPEX optimization through POS SM's e-POS platform

### Medium-Term (2027–2030)
- Potential diversification into **CO₂ carriers** — Korea has been active in CCUS project development, and Pan Ocean's bulk-to-specialty transition mirrors the broader Korean shipowner strategy
- Possible **LNG bunkering vessel fleet expansion** in Asia-Pacific (growing LNG bunkering demand at Korean, Japanese, and Chinese ports)
- **Ammonia carrier** opportunity — as Korea accelerates ammonia imports for power generation under its hydrogen economy strategy

### Key Strategic Tension
Pan Ocean is a mid-sized shipowner relative to global LNG majors (Nakilat, MOL, NYK). Its competitive positioning depends on **OPEX efficiency** (POS SM's core mandate) and **long-term charter security** (oil major relationships). For Benoit, emphasizing how BV services can directly support OPEX control and charter compliance (e.g., in-water surveys, remote survey options, CII optimization) will resonate strongly with POS SM's management team.

## 6. Who Will Be in the Room — Newbuilding Team Context

POS SM's newbuilding team will be focused on:

- **Contract specifications** for next LNG carrier orders (CCS type, engine, BOG management system, notations)
- **Regulatory compliance planning** — especially IGC Code revision (2028), CII, EU ETS
- **Long-term fuel strategy** — how to future-proof newbuilds for ammonia, biofuels, CO₂
- **Shipyard relationship management** — mostly SHI and Hanwha Ocean

Pan Ocean's scale means they likely **follow, rather than pioneer** technical innovation — but they are highly engaged with technical selection decisions because OPEX efficiency is the POS SM mandate. BV's **SEECAT modelling tool** and **early technical engagement in the design phase** are directly relevant offerings.
