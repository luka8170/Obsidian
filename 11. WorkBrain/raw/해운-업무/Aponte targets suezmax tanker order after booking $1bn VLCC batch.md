Shipowner Gianluigi Aponte mulls order for suezmax tankers

![MSC chairman Gianluigi Aponte and Hengli Group chairman Chen Jianhua August 2024.](https://image.dngroup.com/global/nhst/binary/10df00024ec5800bde1e8decd9269d99?crop=1498%2C999%2Cx160%2Cy1%2Csafe&width=1300&format=auto&quality=80)

MSC chairman Gianluigi Aponte and Hengli Group chairman Chen Jianhua August 2024.

Container shipping tycoon Gianluigi Aponte looks to have secured a near $1bn VLCC newbuilding deal and is now understood to be exploring a move for suezmax tankers.

Aponte’s MSC Mediterranean Shipping Company is said to have splashed out about $952m to order eight VLCCs at Hengli Heavy Industry.

News of MSC’s interest in ordering large oil tankers was first reported in TradeWinds earlier this year, as reports emerged that the liner company was partnering with Sinokor Maritime to take a dominant position in the VLCC space.

Then, MSC was said to be in discussion with the Chinese shipyard for up to 10 newbuildings comprising six firm ships plus an option for an additional four.

MSC and Hengli have been contacted for comment.

The price for the scrubber-fitted 306,000-dwt newbuildings has yet to emerge, with brokers suggesting a $119m-per-ship figure.

Hengli is reportedly preparing to deliver the octet between late 2028 and 2029.

Sources say MSC’s interest in tanker newbuildings appears far from over.

The company is believed to be in discussions with the same shipyard for a series of suezmaxes. The number of vessels under consideration has not been disclosed.

One industry player said he is not surprised that MSC is “targeting” suezmaxes as Sinokor is said to be on a secondhand buying spree in the segment.

The Seoul-based company was reported to have set its sights on 15 or more suezmaxes and has already bought five, TradeWinds reported last month.

MSC is an existing customer of Hengli. The company has 28 LNG dual-fuelled ultra-large container ships on order there.

The 24,000-teu series is estimated to cost close to $5.9bn based on the per-vessel price of about $210m.

Clarksons shows Hengli is scheduled to deliver the first container ship in March 2027.

Hengli disclosed that during the first quarter of this year, it contracted a total of 108 newbuildings, including 76 tankers.

Hengli outlined that it has contracted 54 VLCCs, 18 suezmax tankers and four aframax product carriers. The rest of the orders involved 12 container ships, 16 bulk carriers and four VLGCs.

In the same period, it delivered 14 ships ahead of schedule.

Industry players said Hengli’s boss, Chen Jianhua, harbours ambitions to transform the shipyard into a global leader.

However, some expressed concern that Hengli has been slow in issuing refund guarantees for its contracted newbuildings.

They added that the yard is behind on deliveries, having completed only half of its 30 scheduled ships for 2025, and is also significantly behind its 2026 schedule.

By comparison, Clarksons’ data shows that Hengli delivered four vessels in 2024 and 17 ships in 2025.

Hengli was only established in late 2022, when Hengli Group acquired the former STX Dalian Shipbuilding to turn it into an offshore manufacturing base and shipbuilding yard.

In less than four years, Hengli has accumulated an orderbook of nearly 280 newbuildings, according to Clarksons’ records.

Some of the world’s biggest and most influential shipowners have ordered newbuildings there.

Among them are John Fredriksen’s Seatankers, Evangelos Marinakis’ Capital Maritime Group, George Procopiou’s Dynacom Tankers, George Economou’s TMS Tankers, Thanassis Laskaridis’ Alimia Group and Idan Ofer’s Eastern Pacific Shipping.

Dynacom has inked a total of 43 newbuildings there, while Capital has 30 vessels on order.
