## Bricolage

Sometimes we wait to start taking action because we feel we dont have
the resources we need. But there is an abundance of evidence to sug-
gest that successful innovators do just the opposite, getting started with
what they have rather than waiting till the conditions are per fect. Bri
colage is a French word coming from the medieval verb for “to fddle
or tinker ”; sociologists use it to describe how innovators use what they
have on hand to do something. Bricolage is closely elated to one of
the most prominent explanations of how entrepreneurs succeed, a pro-
cess called effectuation, the basic principles being that you: (1) pilot
the plane (you can make your own opportunities by influencing the fu-
ture), (2) use the bird in the hand (like bricolage, make do with what
resources you have), (3) make lemonade out of lemons (do what you can
with surprises), and (4) embrace the crazy quilt (hind value in having a
variety of partnerships).' Although bricolage and effectuation differ in
minor ways (some claim that bricolage is a resource logic and effectu-
ation a decision-making logic), they basically say the same thing: make
the most of what you have and just get started!

#### Taking Small Steps Like a Great Chef

The Stanford researchers concluded that the best way to pursue a new
project in an uncertain environment is to take small steps, switching
between different demands rather than trying to do everything at once.
They suggest tocusing on the most critical area (and using backstops for
the others) until it is good enough-though not necessarily perfect-
and then switching to the next most critical area. It is a bit Ike prepar-
ing a meal. A great chef doesn't try to stir every pot at the exact same
time, nor finish one dish to perfection and then put it aside to spoil
while perfecting the next dish. Instead, they juggle the tasks in a series
of small steps. The most critical tasks are on the front burner, the less
critical items are on the back burner, and the chef rotates them when the
one in front is good enough (as opposed to finished), and so on, weaving
together a meal.
