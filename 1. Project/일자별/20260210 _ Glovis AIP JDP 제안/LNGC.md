With regards to the specific question of JDP/AIP’s to propose, please find some ideas that are more realistic and solutions available on short to mid-term.

- An interesting JDP/AIP would be related to propulsion systems benchmark for which collaboration with OEM’s is needed (potentially also yards). This could be combined with hull/appendices optimization by CFD but both work streams are independent. Wind Propulsion Systems (WPS) may also be integrated in the project.

This type of projects would involve SEECAT (BVS).

**P&C**: In a week time we will release some news about a collaboration project with Wartsila on their Hyel concept for LNG carriers. Additional phases of this Warstila project may be proposed with additional stakeholders such as owners and/or yards.

- A hot topic currently for gas carriers is the WPS, including installation at newbuilding or retrofit stage for any type of system, sails, rotors, etc. It is considered a main trend and there have been some similar JDP/AIP projects for LPG and LNG carriers.

- Methane emissions is still a very relevant topic, including engines methane slip. 

- At Industrial & Commodities Product Line we can work on LNG carrier on-board measurements for methane leaks. LDAR (Leak Detection and Repair) service is currently proposed.
- In M&O certification of engines on test bench and/or onboard is another interesting area.

- I would also propose a JDP to look at the interaction, technical and commercial aspect or ideas around the 174 and/or 200k LNG carrier designs with 3 tanks. A shipyard and GTT may be invited to contribute to the design part of such concept.

- On the LPG segment, a JDP to address the flexibility of propulsion part MFO/MDO, Biofuel, LPG, Ammonia could be an interesting idea for VLGC carriers.

- Less mature in terms of technology readiness level and logistics around offloading is the Onboard Carbon Capture. However, this may also be a subject to cover in such type of project with a yard for instance. This could be applicable to both LNG and LPG carriers and in fact the only gas carrier globally in the world equipped with such technology is a Mid-Size LPG carrier.

- If Glovis is interested to become LNG bunkering provider, assumed part of their PCTC fleet would be LNG fuelled, new propulsion technologies again with hybrid solutions, battery packs, PTI, etc. is a relevant option to consider in an LNG Bunkering Vessel concept design. This type of projects would also involve SEECAT (BVS).

I am sure that there may be also very relevant ideas by the client that you will find in an open discussion. I hope the above ideas will help developing such discussions.
