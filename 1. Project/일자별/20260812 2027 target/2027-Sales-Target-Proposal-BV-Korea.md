## 1. Executive Summary

This document proposes the 2027 sales targets for BV Korea Marine & Offshore, based on a realistic assessment of the 2026 performance baseline, current organizational capacity, and sustainable business development potential.

A key principle underpinning this proposal is that **target growth must be proportional to sales capacity**. BV Korea's current Business Development headcount is critically limited, and the absence of a dedicated Key Account (KA) manager creates a structural gap that cannot be compensated by individual effort alone. Setting ambitious revenue targets without a corresponding increase in sales resources would produce targets that are aspirational in appearance but structurally unachievable — and would therefore undermine the credibility of the target-setting process itself.

## 3. One-Time Items — Sinokor VLCC CAC (Exclusion Rationale)

### 3.1 Transaction Summary

In January 2026, BV Korea secured the Change of Authorized Certifier (CAC) for up to **30 VLCC vessels** (approx. 4.8M GT) being purchased by Sinokor, in competition with KR (Korean Register). This was a landmark win for BV Korea's market position.

However, several structural characteristics of this transaction make it **inappropriate to include in the 2027 baseline**:

| Factor | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Non-recurring nature** | This was a fleet acquisition event, not a recurring contract. Sinokor's VLCC purchasing activity of this scale is not expected to repeat annually. |
| **Internal fee sharing arrangement** | BV Korea's actual recognized revenue from this deal was substantially reduced by the internal fee-sharing mechanism (fees capped/limited by BV Singapore and BV China per HQ authorization). CD Pusan was projected to **absorb a local loss** on these vessels. |
| **Survey location** | Most periodic surveys are performed in Singapore and China (APA region), not Korea. BV Korea's marginal benefit is limited to the KA relationship and initial CAC administration. |
| **Market distortion risk** | Using this exceptional transaction as a baseline would inflate 2027 targets by an amount that cannot be replicated through normal BD activity. |

## 4. Organizational Capacity Assessment

### 4.1 Current Sales Headcount

| Role | Status | Impact |
|------|--------|--------|
| Business Development Manager | 1 FTE (Sangjin Han) | Primary BD and KA responsibility |
| Key Account Manager | **VACANT** | No dedicated KA coverage |
| KPO (Korea Port Office) | 1 FTE (Young-Jun Seo) | Technical/survey support, partial BD |
| NS/BVN | 1 FTE (Bong-Seok Kang) | Survey operations |

**Critical finding:** BV Korea currently has **no dedicated Key Account Manager**. With the Sinokor account now representing a major portion of BV Korea's strategic portfolio — and requiring active relationship management across fleet operations, claims (lashing claim), survey coordination, and potential NC pipeline — the absence of a KA owner is a direct business risk.

### 4.2 Capacity Constraints and Risk

**One BD manager cannot realistically:**
- Actively manage 1 major KA (Sinokor, 30+ VLCC + growing fleet)
- Pursue new FYF agreements (Ventus/Njord, Panstar, Hana Marine, Hyundai LNG Shipping)
- Lead NC business development (HMM, Polaris, LNGC pipelines)
- Attend industry events, conduct proactive outreach
- Handle internal coordination (HQ, APA, fee-sharing, claims)
- Meet a materially higher revenue target simultaneously

Setting a substantially higher 2027 revenue target **without adding sales headcount is not a stretch goal — it is an unfunded mandate.**
