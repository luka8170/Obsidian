

The following details the core theoretical background and industry benchmark cases supporting BV Korea's position to reject HQ's proposal to expand the Key Account (KA) list and maintain only 'Sinokor Maritime (Tankers)' as the sole KA.

## 1. Strict Conditions for KAM (Homburg, Workman & Jensen, 2002)

This theory posits the foundational criterion that **"not just any customer can be a Key Account."** According to research by Homburg et al., for a company to designate a specific customer as a KA and allocate corporate resources, the following **three conditions must be met simultaneously**:

- **(1) Significant Revenue:** The account must represent a scale that significantly impacts the company's current or near-future profits.
    
- **(2) Multi-dimensional Approach:** The customer must be complex and important enough to require cross-functional coordination (technology, sales, executive management, etc.), not just a response from a single sales representative.
    
- **(3) Existing Relationship:** There must already be a **'substantial business relationship'** in place to justify prioritized investment at the corporate level.
    

**[Application to BV Korea]** Many of the Korean shipowners proposed as KAs by HQ (e.g., Pan Ocean, HMM) currently hold a 0% BV market share. Thus, condition (3), 'Existing Relationship', is entirely absent. According to this theory, customers with a 0% share are not KAs deserving of massive resource allocation, but rather 'Prospects/Target Accounts' where a relationship needs to be initiated through general sales efforts. Currently, the only account meeting all three conditions is the Sinokor Maritime Tanker division, which accounts for a 29% share (106 vessels).

## 2. KAM Relationship Development Stage Model (Napolitano / Millman-Wilson)

This model explains how the relationship between a supplier and a customer evolves over time, asserting that **"a KAM system is only effective beyond a certain stage of the relationship."**

- **Evolution by Stage:** Pre-KAM (No existing transactions) → Early KAM (Initial transactions) → Mid-KAM (Regular transactions and trust building) → Partnership KAM → Synergistic KAM
    
- **Core Theory:** Massive KAM resource investments (dedicated personnel, in-depth reporting, customized strategies, etc.) are only justified at the **Mid-KAM stage or higher**, where mutual understanding and trust have accumulated. Forcing KAM onto the Pre-KAM stage results in severe opportunity costs and wasted resources.
    

**[Application to BV Korea]** The 0% share accounts HQ intends to add all fall under the lowest relationship tier: **'Pre-KAM'**. Academic literature indicates that forcing an advanced KAM approach onto Pre-KAM customers is illogical, leading to wasted resources and lost opportunity costs (Premature Failure). At this stage, what is required is not complex paperwork (KAP), but 'Business Development' driven by fieldwork.

## 3. The Selection Paradox and the Fallacy of Resource Dispersion (McDonald & Woodburn, 2007)

This study, often considered the bible of the KAM field, warns against the **fatal side effects of 'excessive KA designation.'**

- **The Selection Paradox:** "Designating too many key accounts is the most common mistake in KAM implementation. If everyone is a Key Account, no one is."
    
- **Physical Limits:** When there are too many critical customers to manage, the sales organization's focus becomes diluted and resources are dispersed, ultimately degrading the quality of service provided to the truly important customers (core revenue generators).
    
- **Recommended Governance:** It is most effective to limit KAs to a maximum of 15–20 for a mid-sized sales team, and 5–10 for a highly focused program.
    

**[Application to BV Korea]** Currently, the marine sales team at the Korea branch consists of only 5 members. If they are forced to add 15 or more new KAs as directed by HQ—taking on KAP, Account Plans, and HOCT reporting—the paperwork will increase exponentially. This will inevitably lead to poor management of Sinokor Maritime, the only definitive core customer, creating a 'counter-effect' that threatens our existing, secure revenue stream.

## 4. Industry Benchmarks: KAM Operations of Leading Companies

Beyond academic theory, leading companies in marine classification and other industries operate highly selective and intensive KAM programs under strict criteria. HQ's proposal for an indiscriminate expansion of KAs directly contradicts these industry standards.

### 4.1 DNV (Tiered Account Management by the #1 Marine Classification Society)

DNV, BV's closest competitor, operates a formal 3-tier account system.

- **System Structure:** Global Key Account (~15-20), Regional Key Account, Prospecting Account.
    
- **Entry Criteria:** KAM protocols are _never_ applied to accounts without existing transactions. The criteria for Regional KAM designation are strictly limited to **a minimum of 10% fleet share (or at least 5 classified vessels)**. This is not a mere administrative guideline; it is the minimum threshold proving that 'a meaningful, practical business relationship and trust' (such as vessel surveys and technical support) has been formed. Accounts falling below this threshold (e.g., 0% share) are classified into the 'Prospecting Account' (Business Development) track, preventing the waste of resources on complex KAM paperwork and allowing teams to focus on field sales.
    

### 4.2 Lloyd's Register (Account Selection Based on the Pareto Principle)

- **Selective Focus:** LR applies a modified Pareto principle, where **only the top 20% of accounts** (based on revenue and strategic value) are subject to formal KAM, while the remaining 80% fall under general account management.
    
- **Core Principle:** KAM investments are made only when justified by existing relationships and revenue. Including a 0% share account in KAM violates this foundational rule.
    

### 4.3 SAP (2018 Restructuring to Overcome Excessive KA Designation)

- **Structural Reform (2018):** Realizing that excessive KA designation was weakening performance, SAP drastically reduced its Global KA list **from over 200 to just 35**.
    
- **Mandatory Dedicated Personnel:** They retained only accounts with strong existing relationships and clear growth potential, making the assignment of a dedicated Account Executive mandatory. They concluded that KAM without dedicated personnel is merely a 'label'. Accounts not meeting the critical threshold were separated into a distinct Prospecting Track.
    

### 4.4 Kongsberg Maritime (Clear Ownership-Based KAM)

- **1 Account = 1 Owner:** They strictly enforce a structural prerequisite: '1 Key Account = 1 Named Account Owner'. If there is no Account Owner, the Key Account status is not granted.
    
- **Track Separation:** Target Accounts aimed at expanding market share (Penetration strategy) are strictly separated into a distinct Business Development Track, rather than being managed under KAM.
    

### Summary and Conclusion

Director Sangjin Han is not simply protesting that "there is too much work."

He points out that **"HQ's current proposal violates the basic, academically proven principles of KAM,"** and thoroughly demonstrates—through academic theories and **empirical benchmark cases from marine classification competitors (DNV, LR) and global leaders (SAP, Kongsberg)**—that merely attaching the 'Key Account' label to a customer with a 0% market share, without dedicated managers or clear KPIs, will not improve performance.

Therefore, the only theoretically and practically sound alternative is to focus intact KAM resources solely on 'Sinokor Maritime (Tankers)', which possesses both an existing relationship and growth potential, and to separate the remaining zero-share customers into a distinct Business Development track.
