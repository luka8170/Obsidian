Dominant global VLCC owner Sinokor Maritime and its partner MSC Mediterranean Shipping Co have struck again in the secondhand market.

Taiwanese owner Sincere Navigation has revealed another sale to the group in a stock exchange filing.

The scrubber-fitted 297,000-dwt Maxim (built 2011) has gone for a very firm $82.5m, with MSC given as the buyer.

Gianluigi Aponte-controlled MSC is in the process of buying a 50% stake in Sinokor after helping fund its massive VLCC acquisition spree since December.

The Sincere ship has been acquired for forward delivery in the first quarter of 2027.

VesselsValue assesses the VLCC, which operates in the Tankers International VLCC pool, as worth $79.38m.

Sinokor has been contacted for comment.

The Maxim has been owned by the Taiwanese company since delivery.

Sincere said it will book a profit of $9.82m from the deal.

The company received valuations from London brokers Arrow at $82m and Clarksons at $81m.

In March, Sincere confirmed the sale of the 296,714-dwt VLCC Kondor (built 2012) for $78.4m. Sinokor was linked to this ship as well.

Sinokor’s purchase drive has seen dozens of ships change hands and asset values spike.

VesselsValue lists 80 VLCCs in its fleet, with 14 large tankers still to be delivered.

Brokers have said the South Korean owner controls between 20% and 25% of the mainstream VLCC fleet.

Clarksons lists it with 247 ships in a mixed fleet, plus 10 on order.

Sincere has three other VLCCs built in 2017 and 2018.

Greek broker Xclusiv said tanker sale-and-purchase activity remains relatively quiet.

Greece’s Posidonia gathering last week had slowed deal-making.

Eva Tzima, head of research and valuations at Greek broker Cass Technava, said there are signs the tanker sector’s returns will fail to revisit the “jaw-dropping levels of spring”.

“Investment focus continues to pivot from the secondhand market, where most candidates are still being marketed at levels corresponding to much higher profitability,” she added.

Attention is switching to newbuilding investment instead, Tzima believes, with 60% to 70% of the cost able to be easily financed by the sale of a 15-year-old tanker in the case of VLCCs.

“The argument in favour of newbuildings, though, is not only one related to price,” she added.

Much later deliveries that would be otherwise considered to carry greater risk are offering enough time for market dynamics to be restored through an expected scrapping cycle.
