Dear Roberto,

The undersigned and my assistant Ludan li met with Madam Yang and her colleague Ms. Zhang (hereafter refer to “the Project Group’) as scheduled yesterday morning. The meeting started from 9:30 am and ended close to the noon time.

The Project Group firstly introduced the purpose of their research project (hereafter refer to “the Project’) and the status of their research and investigation. Madam Yang mentioned Chinese shippers had long been complaining about the various surcharges to related government authorities, and according to the SSE’ s calculation on its filing system, they found that there were more than 200 kinds of surcharges which are filed by shipping lines. The MOT together with SDRC therefore designated the SSE to investigate and study the surcharge matter. This is the first time for the MOT to require a unit to do such kind Project on surcharge matter. The Project would last for 2 years from the middle of 2012 to the middle of 2014. At the first stage of the Project, they had conducted investigation on shippers, shipping lines, some shipper associations which locate in the port cities such as Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Xiamen, Dalian, Tianjin, Qingdao.The preliminary report had been completed and submitted to the MOT at the beginning of this year. MOT gave some comments and suggestions on the preliminary report and required them to further investigate and accordingly make some amendments to the preliminary report. Now the SSE launches the medium-term stage of the investigation. SSE would hold a celebration on its 10 years' anniversary in late November of this year when they would issue an abstract of this Project in one of the bulletin published by SSE. At the end of this year, they would invite some experts to review and assess this Project. The final report of the Project would be submitted to the MOT after the expert meeting and SDRC for their consideration. Whether the final report of this Project would be disclosed to the public is left to the MOT’s decision.

After the Project Group’s introduction, we introduced to them that we had been appointed as the point of contract and representatives by 12 Agreements since 2007 and we, on behalf of the Agreements, have been consulting with the CSA on the rates and surcharges decided by the Agreements and filed them with the MOT ever since. We have also maintained regular contact with the MOT seeking their comment and suggestion on our work in respect of the consultation and the filing of the documents. So far the MOT feels satisfactory about our work. Since we acted as the representatives of many Agreements and had good understanding and knowledge on container transportation, the MOT had ever invited the undersigned as one expert to review and assess several projects relating to monopoly and competition on liner conferences done by Shanghai Maritime University and Dalian Maritime University. For the Project, we told the Project Group that we would be very happy to communicate with them on what we understood and provide them with information and documents if they need. Since the time was limited for us to collect enough information from the Agreements and individual shipping lines under the Agreements we present, this time we could only provide them with the agreements’ preliminary views. When we got more information from the Agreements and individual shipping lines, we could arrange another meeting with them or supplement our further opinion and view in writing. The Project Group answered they would consider whether a further meeting is necessary, but a written explanation is of course welcome. Then we began to discuss with them on the listed topics as follows:

On the nature of GRI, we presented your suggested reply to them holding the view that the GRI only related to ocean freight rates, not surcharges. We asked them why they considered GRI should include the surcharge. They told us that there were some shipping lines having filed GRI as surcharge with the SSE. Also, some Chines shippers also paid GRI as surcharge to the lines. They did not think it right for the lines to collect the GRI from Chinese shippers for FOB cargoes who had no contractual relationship with shipping lines. If considering GRI as ocean freight rate, shipping lines should not collect it from Chinese shippers for FOB cargoes. The Project Group also confused that if GRI was deemed as ocean freight rate, why shipping lines did not file the total freight rate post GRI, but just file a GRI quantum. For instance, if the current freight rate is USD2000/TEU, then they should file USD2500/TEU when GRI was USD500/TEU. However, in practice shipping lines only filed USD500/TEU of GRI with the SSE which was likely to cause shippers to consider it as surcharge. We clarified that the Agreement always hold the view that GRI (may be called by Rate Restoration (RR) by some Agreements ) was ocean freight rate and also filed it as ocean freight rat with the MOT. Since the base freight rates were different among shipping lines, the Agreements could not file the freight rate post GRI and they could only reach a GRI to be recommended to individual shipping lines.

We asked The Project Group that who made the complaint to MOT/SSE telling them shipping lines collected the GRI as surcharges from Chinese shippers for FOB cargoes. The Project Group advised that prior to meeting with us, they conducted investigation with the CSA and Mr. Cai Jiaxiang who is the vice-chairman of the CSA, complained the above to The Project Group. I told The Project Group that Mr. Cai always held prejudice on shipping lines and is a man of strong personality through contact with him on some occasions. In this regard, I thought his viewpoint on the GRI matter was wrong. I suggested the Project Group should find evidences like the invoice, receipt or other documents which shippers had paid to prove the fact. In the meantime, we would ask the Agreement to check whether there was shipping lines to collect GRI in the form of surcharge or to file it as surcharge.

On the THC matter, I explained to the Project Group that I got involved in the whole process of that THC investigation from 2002 and 2006. In terms of the 2006 THC Decision, the THC was not forbidden and charging the THC was very common in the world. We also mentioned the rename of Shanghai Port Surcharge into Shanghai THC and the change of currency in late 2009 which was not related to the quantum at that time.I stressed that since then, the Agreements had not been involved in any resolutions/recommendations on THC adjustments and left the THC to be decided by individual members. Moreover, I told them the reason for individual shipping lines to collect the THC was because the ports had increased the terminal charge beforehand.

For the consultation mechanism, we explained to the Project Group in detail how we consulted with the CSA on increase or adjustment of freight rates and surcharges and filed them with the MOT. The Agreements usually gave 30 days to the CSA to consider the consultation item and put forward their opinion. However, in extraordinary circumstances like surcharge for port congestion, since it was very urgent, the Agreements may give them a shorter time to consider.

The Agreements always insisted on fully and effective consultation with the CSA. For the above rename of Shanghai Port Surcharge matter, the TSA Executive Administrator, legal counsel in America and legal counsel in China flied to Shanghai to consult with the 8 shippers’ Associations, indicating that the Agreements always attached importance to consult with Chinese shippers. However, we advised to the Project Group that so far, the CSA had never indicated their agreement to any of our consulted item and put forward the nearly the same objection opinion no matter what consultation items were. Nonetheless, we always gave detailed explanation to CSA on their objection option and offered to meet them if they so required. We also told the Project Group that we heard that the consultation mechanism between shippers and shipping lines in Japan worked well and we hoped a good consultation mechanism could be established in China if CSA had the sincerity to do so.

We were surprised to hear from the Project Group that Mr. Cai of the CSA told her that the Agreements had not conducted a proper consultation with the CSA so far, just giving a notice to CSA before implementing a new rate or a surcharge. We explained to them that that was not the case, and showed them the sample of filing documents we had field with the MOT indicating to them about them about our normal practice regarding the consultation and filing. Upon our explanations and reading the sample of the filing documents, the Project Group also considered that we had conducted proper consultation with the CSA and showed disagreement to Mr. Cai’s views. We told the Project Group that we thought the CSA should be the representative of Chinese shippers which was agreed in 2007 when the Circular of Strengthening the Supervision on Liner Conference and Rate Discussion was issued. However, during the consultation on the rename of Shanghai Port Surcharge matter, the CSA complained that we should also consult with local shippers’ association. We expressed that it was difficult for the Agreements to do so since there were many kinds of such associations in China. We also conveyed your positions on the status of the CSA to the Project Group. Madam Yang requested for a copy of several our consultation and filing letters with CSA and MOT, and we gave her a copy.

On the RMB local surcharges, we conveyed your comments to the Project Group advising that the Agreements did not address most of the RMB local surcharges, except WTSA had ever charged a Document Fee in South China in 2009. For the consultation mechanism, we held the same view with that in the above THC matter.

With respect to the reason and procedure of adding new surcharges or adjustment to an old surcharge, we present your comments to the Project Group on this topic. Currently the Agreements filed the FAF and CAF with the MOT in China and only under extraordinary circumstances like war, strike, unrest or port congestions, the Agreements may recommend a new surcharge. And we also mentioned that all the decisions recommended by the Agreements were voluntary and non-binding and members were free to depart from them. We reminded the Project Group that the loose nature of the Agreements we represent were different from that of liner conference and other monopoly organization. We learn from our daily work that when discussing an adjustment to rate or surcharge or initiating a new surcharge, members often hold different views based to their individual commercial strategy and operation situation. The members would finally compromise each other to reach a resolution. Though the resolution was finally arrived, there were still some members which mitigated it or did not implement such resolution.

We told the Project Group that though the EU removed the anti-monopoly exemption for liner conference and rate discussion agreement, we learnt that there was cut-throat competition in the Europe trade lanes and freight rate were very volatile and even some shippers required resuming the anti-monopoly exemption. Due to the extreme fluctuation of freight rate, shippers had poor predictability of their profit and were hard for them to manage their costs. The agreements’ existences can provide relative stable freight rates for shippers and address the forming of oligopoly. That was the reasons why other countries did not follow the step of the EU. And we advised the Project Group that, to our knowledge, the MOT was not in favor of removing anti-monopoly exemption in China.

For the reason of split of surcharge from freight rate, we also conveyed your comments to them when they questioned why some surcharges were long existed once implementing.

 
On the related legal explanations on the surcharge matter, we mentioned to the Project Group that it seemed that the UN Liner Conference Code had a definition on this and there were no laws and regulations in China to make a definition on surcharge. As provided by the filing circular, freight rate was market-regulated price, we thought that how to collect the freight rate upon shippers should be decided by shipping lines and government should not interfere with private commercial activities. We also mentioned your comments to the Project Group. The government should interfere to the activities of shipping lines only when they were in breach of the law such as abuse of their dominant position. The new Administrations led by the Prime Minister Li Keqiang has stressed the importance of the market driven economy. Since Mr Li took office from this March, the Central Government has cancelled hundred of administrative permits in order to reduce the governmental interference into the economy as much as possible.

Finally, the Project Group thanked our useful and comprehensive comments and suggestions for the Project. We also told them if further comments and suggestions were required we would be willing to provide with them.

In short, we think it is beneficial and effective communication with the Project Group, on one hand, we presented the agreements' position to them clearly; on the other hand,  we had a chance to clarify some rumors against shipping lines such as the CSA’s viewpoints.

If you have any comment on the above report, please feel free to let us know.
