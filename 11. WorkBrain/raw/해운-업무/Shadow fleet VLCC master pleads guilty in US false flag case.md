The Georgian master of a shadow tanker seized by the US has pleaded guilty to a criminal charge arising from the case.

The Department of Justice (DoJ) said in a statement that Avtandil Kalandadze, 47, formerly of the 318,500-dwt VLCC Marinera (ex-Bella 1, built 2002), admitted in US District Court that he refused to obey orders from the US Coast Guard during a multi-week pursuit from the Caribbean Sea to the northern Atlantic.

The DoJ said the vessel was a “ghost fleet” tanker that historically transported Iranian and Venezuelan oil for the ultimate benefit of US adversaries.

The Georgian was charged in February over his refusal to allow a US military team on board, sparking an 11-knot (20 km per hour) pursuit across the Atlantic.

US military teams seized the ship off the coast of Iceland on 7 January and detained the crew after US President Donald Trump’s order to blockade Venezuela’s oil exports.

Kalandadze tried to resist seizure by falsely claiming the VLCC was flying the flag of Guyana.

The US claimed that as a result, the Marinera could be boarded by a navy under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.

“Kalandadze’s guilty plea today should serve as a warning to ghost fleet owners and operators and all those who man or otherwise support these dangerous, decrepit vessels,” said John Eisenberg, the assistant attorney general for national security.

“The Department of Justice and our interagency partners will pursue the ghost fleet and its facilitators from the Caribbean Sea to the North Atlantic, to the Indian and Pacific Oceans, the Persian Gulf, and anywhere in between. You will not escape.”

US attorney Jeanine Ferris Pirro, for the District of Columbia, said the master had put American sailors’ and Coast Guardsmen’s lives at risk while attempting to evade US sanctions and move illicit oil.

“By refusing lawful orders from the US Coast Guard and leading them on a chase across the Atlantic Ocean, he not only obstructed federal law enforcement, but forced our service members to carry out a dangerous boarding operation in heavy seas.”

The captain used a range of “deceptive maritime tactics”, the DoJ statement added.

Sentencing was set for 7 August.

The charge carries a maximum penalty of five years in prison.

Kalandadze will be deported at the completion of his prison term.

According to the plea agreement, he was the captain of the tanker from September until late December 2025.

During this time, the ship transported about 1.8m barrels of Iran-origin oil to Asia.

“Kalandadze employed common obfuscation techniques to hide the activities of the Bella 1, including sailing with an inactivated automatic identification system (AIS) and concealing the name of the Bella 1 while engaging in a ship-to-ship transfer of the Iran-origin oil to another vessel,” the DoJ said.

The US-sanctioned ship was listed as false-flagged by shipping databases and followed a warning from Guyana’s maritime administration about the abuse of its registry.

The ship switched from Turkish to Russian ownership after the initial attempt at boarding, according to Equasis.

It was also reflagged to Russia, with the crew repurposing a Dutch flag to fly on the ship.

The remainder of the crew, which included Russians, Ukrainians, Indians and Georgians, were taken ashore in Scotland before being flown home or the US.
