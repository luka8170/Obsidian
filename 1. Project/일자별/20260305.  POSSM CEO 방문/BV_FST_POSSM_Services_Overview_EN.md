## 1. About BV Future Ship Team (FST)

Bureau Veritas' Future Ship Team (FST) is a dedicated global center of excellence focused exclusively on helping shipowners and operators navigate the energy transition. Headquartered across Singapore and Shanghai, the FST brings together naval architects, marine engineers, fuel technology specialists, regulatory experts, and data scientists to deliver fully integrated decarbonization advisory services.

The visiting expert, based in BV's Shanghai office, brings hands-on experience from one of the most complex and large-scale alternative fuel conversion programs in the world — the **Carnival Cruise fleet transition project** — and has since led multiple feasibility and roadmap engagements across bulk carriers, tankers, and container vessels globally.

The FST operates at the intersection of three critical domains:

- **Technical:** alternative fuel system design, retrofit engineering, safety assessment
- **Regulatory:** IMO, EU ETS, FuelEU, MARPOL compliance and strategy
- **Commercial:** lifecycle cost modeling, operational impact analysis, investor-grade reporting

## 2. Core Service Offerings

### 2-1. Fleet Decarbonization Roadmap

**What it is:**  
A structured, data-driven strategy document that charts a shipowner's entire fleet from its current GHG intensity baseline to a defined future target — whether that is CII rating improvement, net-zero by 2050, or alignment with a specific charterer or financial institution's requirements.

**What it covers:**
- Baseline fleet carbon intensity assessment (CII, EEXI, AER)
- Scenario modeling across short (2025–2027), medium (2028–2035), and long-term (2036–2050) horizons
- Fuel pathway comparison: LNG, methanol, ammonia, hydrogen, biofuels, and e-fuels
- Prioritization of vessels by retrofit feasibility, operational profile, and remaining useful life
- Financial impact modeling including CAPEX, OPEX, fuel price sensitivity, and EU ETS cost exposure
- Carbon budget allocation and fleet replacement scheduling

**Why it matters for POSSM:**  
As POSSM transitions its portfolio from dry bulk into the wet segment (tankers), the company is at an inflection point where strategic decisions made now — on fuel type, propulsion technology, and classification requirements — will define operational and regulatory competitiveness for the next 20+ years. A roadmap built at this stage allows POSSM to align newbuilding specifications with long-term fuel availability and regulatory requirements rather than retrofitting later at higher cost.

### 2-2. Alternative Fuel Feasibility Study

**What it is:**  
A rigorous, vessel-specific technical and commercial evaluation of alternative fuel options, comparing each across multiple dimensions before a newbuilding order is placed or a retrofit decision is made.

**Evaluation dimensions:**
- **Technical compatibility:** Engine compatibility (dual-fuel, mono-fuel), tank sizing, piping modifications, onboard safety systems (FGSS — Fuel Gas Supply System)
- **Fuel supply & bunkering infrastructure:** Availability by port, regional supply maturity, supply chain risk
- **Safety profile:** Toxicity, flammability, energy density trade-offs — particularly relevant for ammonia
- **Regulatory compliance:** IGF Code, IGC Code, interim guidelines for ammonia/hydrogen, flag state requirements
- **Economic analysis:** Total cost of ownership over vessel lifecycle, bunker price forecasting, carbon credit impact
- **Operational impact:** Range, cargo capacity reduction, crew training requirements

**Fuel types covered:**
| Fuel | Maturity | BV FST Experience |
|---|---|---|
| LNG | Commercial | Extensive — IGF Code certification, FSRU/LNG bunkering |
| Bio-LNG / Bio-methane | Emerging | Drop-in compatibility analysis |
| Methanol | Growing | Maersk, Stena retrofit experience |
| Ammonia | Pilot stage | Safety framework design, IMO interim guidelines |
| Hydrogen | Early | Fuel cell feasibility, port pilot projects |
| Biofuels (B20/B100) | Immediate | Drop-in studies, engine compatibility checks |

**Why it matters for POSSM:**  
The Vale Newcastlemax tender specifically includes three alternative fuel options as part of its vessel specification requirements. BV FST can provide POSSM with a rapid comparative assessment of all three options under consideration, factoring in the specific operating profile of Newcastlemax vessels and Vale's preferred bunkering ports in Brazil and Southeast Asia.

### 2-3. Alternative Fuel Performance & Emissions Simulation

**What it is:**  
Using BV's proprietary simulation tools and actual voyage data, FST models how a vessel's energy efficiency and GHG emissions will change across different fuel scenarios — providing shipowners with defensible, quantified projections rather than estimates.

**Simulation outputs:**
- CII rating trajectory over a 5–10 year horizon under each fuel scenario
- CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O (well-to-wake) emission calculations aligned with IMO's LCA guidelines
- Fuel consumption and cost projections under different speed and load conditions
- EU ETS compliance cost modeling (2024–2034 phase-in)
- FuelEU Maritime penalty exposure analysis (from 2025 onwards)
- Comparison of operational (speed reduction, CII offset credits) vs. technical (fuel switch, retrofit) measures

**Data inputs used:**
- AIS voyage history and operational profile
- Current fleet CII ratings and EEXI certification data
- Bunker price forecasts (IEA, Platts references)
- Vessel hull form and engine performance parameters

**Why it matters for POSSM:**  
With CII requirements tightening annually and EU ETS now applying to shipping, POSSM needs to understand not just "which fuel is cleaner" but "which fuel makes us CII-compliant by 2027 and ETS-cost-effective by 2030." Simulation allows decisions to be made on actual numbers rather than market perception.

### 2-4. Newbuilding Alternative Fuel Integration Support

**What it is:**  
End-to-end technical advisory and classification support for shipowners ordering new vessels with alternative fuel propulsion systems — from initial design review through sea trials and delivery.

**Scope of support:**
- **Pre-contract stage:** Fuel system specification review, shipyard technical capability assessment, comparative analysis of dual-fuel vs. conventional designs
- **Design approval stage:** Plan approval under BV's alternative fuel rules (NR529, NR670 for methanol, NR671 for ammonia), coordination with flag state, review of FGSS and fuel containment systems
- **Construction stage:** Statutory and class surveys, witnessing of factory acceptance tests for fuel system components
- **Delivery & certification:** EEXI Technical File, CII baseline establishment, fuel system commissioning sign-off
- **Post-delivery:** Operational monitoring, fuel system troubleshooting, ongoing class compliance

**Specific support for the Vale Newcastlemax project:**
- Review of three alternative fuel specification options as outlined in the Vale RFP
- Assessment of Chinese shipyard capability (including New Rongcheng and other candidate yards) for building alternative fuel vessels — leveraging BV Shanghai's direct yard survey network
- Coordination between BV Korea (client relationship), BV China (yard surveys), and BV FST (fuel system expertise)
- Preparation of BV's technical endorsement documentation to support POSSM's tender proposal to Vale

### 2-5. IMO & International Regulatory Compliance Advisory

**What it is:**  
A continuously updated advisory service that translates complex and rapidly evolving international maritime regulations into actionable decisions for fleet managers and technical teams.

**Regulatory areas covered:**

| Regulation | Effective | Key Impact |
|---|---|---|
| EEXI (Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index) | 2023 | One-time technical certification for all vessels 400 GT+ |
| CII (Carbon Intensity Indicator) | 2023, tightening annually | Annual rating A–E; D/E ratings trigger corrective action plans |
| EU ETS (Emissions Trading System) | 2024 | Carbon cost on 100% of EU intra-voyages, 50% of international legs |
| FuelEU Maritime | 2025 | GHG intensity target for fuel used in EU ports and voyages |
| IMO LCA Guidelines (Lifecycle) | 2028 (expected) | Well-to-wake accounting for alternative fuels |
| IMO 2030/2050 GHG Strategy | Ongoing | Indicative checkpoints at 2030 (20–30% reduction) and 2050 (net-zero) |

**Advisory deliverables:**
- Regulatory gap analysis for POSSM's current fleet
- CII corrective action plan drafting (required for vessels rated D or E)
- EU ETS reporting template setup and allowance (EUA) purchasing strategy
- FuelEU Maritime compliance pathway and pooling strategy
- Representation at IMO MEPC sessions briefings (where applicable)

### 2-6. Carbon Accounting & ESG Reporting Support

**What it is:**  
As shipping enters the era of mandatory emissions disclosure — both to regulators (EU MRV, IMO DCS) and to investors/charterers — BV FST supports shipowners in building robust carbon accounting systems and producing credible ESG reports.

**Services:**
- Establishment of vessel-level and fleet-level GHG inventory (Scope 1, 2, 3)
- EU MRV monitoring plan setup and verification
- IMO DCS data collection and reporting
- Alignment with Poseidon Principles and Sea Cargo Charter frameworks (for financing and chartering)
- Preparation of sustainability disclosures for annual reports and investor presentations
- Carbon offset strategy: voluntary credits, biofuel certificate (Book & Claim), and in-sector reduction

**Why it matters for POSSM:**  
As POSSM pursues long-term contracts with major commodity traders (Vale), its carbon reporting credentials will increasingly be scrutinized. Charterers and lenders now routinely require CII ratings, EU MRV verification reports, and sustainability commitments as part of contract and financing terms.

### 2-7. Crew Training & Safety Management for Alternative Fuels

**What it is:**  
Operational safety is the most frequently cited barrier to alternative fuel adoption — particularly for ammonia and hydrogen. BV FST provides a structured program to build crew competency and shipboard safety culture in parallel with the technical transition.

**Program components:**

- **Risk Assessment & HAZID:** Identification of hazardous scenarios specific to each alternative fuel (e.g., ammonia toxicity zones, methanol fire suppression, LNG boil-off management)
- **Safety Management System (SMS) updates:** Integration of alternative fuel handling procedures into the vessel's ISM-compliant SMS
- **Emergency Response Planning:** Fuel leak response, medical protocols for toxic fuel exposure, coordination with port emergency services
- **Crew Competency Framework:** Aligned with IMO STCW requirements and BV's own certification program for alternative fuel handling
- **Tabletop drills & simulation:** Shore-based training scenarios for chief engineers, officers, and ratings
- **Onboard commissioning support:** BV surveyor presence during first bunkering and fuel system operation to guide crew in real conditions
