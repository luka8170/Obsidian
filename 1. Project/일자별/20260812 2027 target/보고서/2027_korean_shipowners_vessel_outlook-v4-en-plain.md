
### In-Depth Check of Newbuilding Orders and Used Ship Purchases

---

To help set the sales target, we checked Korean shipowners' 2027 newbuilding order activity, expected ship deliveries, and used-ship (secondhand) purchase volume against reliable Clarkson Research data.

> **Data source:** Clarkson Research — World Fleet Monitor Vol.17 No.7 (July 2026), and other Clarkson reports kept on Bureau Veritas SharePoint only.
> **Numbers confirmed via:** Bureau Veritas SharePoint > M&O Segments & Market Intelligence > General Market Intelligence Marine

## 1. Summary of Korean Shipowners' 2027 Outlook

| Item                                | 2025 Actual                  | 2026 So Far (H1)                                            | 2027 Outlook                                                     | Direction                                                           |
| :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **New ship orders**                 | 5,588 thousand GT / 85 ships | 3,047 thousand GT / 67 ships (Jan–Jun)                      | About 6,000 thousand GT (yearly-rate basis)                      | **Flat or slightly lower**                                          |
| **Ship deliveries** (already fixed) | —                            | 1,616 thousand GT (H1 actual, 33 ships)                     | **3,900 thousand GT**                                            | About +43.6% vs. 2026 actual+planned total (already-fixed schedule) |
| **Used ship purchases (S&P)**       | 3,226 thousand GT / 39 ships | 10,180 thousand GT / 70 ships (Jan–Jun, world's No.1 buyer) | About 3,000–5,000 thousand GT if it normalizes (similar to 2025) | **Normalization scenario (risk of a sharp drop)**                   |
| **Fleet owned**                     | 72.5 million GT (year-end)   | 83.7 million GT (July snapshot)                             | —                                                                | Keeps growing                                                       |

### Reasons Behind the 2027 Outlook

**① New Orders — Flat or Slightly Lower**
- The 2026 H1 order pace is up +9.0% year-on-year — modest growth — but worry about oversupply from the record wave of deliveries in 2026–2028 is making owners cautious about ordering more
- No official forecast exists, but as long as shipyards stay booked up (already 3+ years of backlog) and newbuilding prices stay high, 2027 orders are expected to land around the 2026 level (~6,000 thousand GT)

**② Used Ship Purchases (S&P) — Normalization Scenario (Could Return to 2025 Level, Risk of a Sharp Drop)**
- 2026 H1 S&P jumped +531.1%, an all-time high — but this was mostly driven by a few large one-off deals, such as SINOKOR's VLCC purchase
- Simply doubling the H1 figure (~20,360 thousand GT) to estimate the full year overstates things, because it bakes in the effect of that one-off deal. Once the deal effect fades, 2027 could fall sharply back to the 2025 level (3,226 thousand GT)
- Demand for eco-friendly used ships — driven by the IMO carbon rules taking effect in 2027 — could soften the drop somewhat, but it's unlikely to offset the size of that one-off deal

**③ Ship Deliveries — Volume Will Rise a Lot, But This Doesn't Mean More Class-Switching Business (Already Fixed)**
- The 3,900 thousand GT figure for 2027 is an already-fixed number from Clarkson's orderbook schedule. Compared with the 2026 full-year total (1,616 thousand GT actually delivered in H1 + 1,100 thousand GT still due in H2 = 2,716 thousand GT), the volume itself is up about +43.6%
- However, Korean shipowners strongly tend to keep the same classification society they chose when placing the newbuilding order, even after delivery. So more deliveries don't automatically mean more new class-society business (CAC)
- Still, it's worth setting a modest CAC growth target based on continuing class work (surveys) and any new registrations under the existing class society

> **What this means for BV Korea:** Korean shipowners' new orders are expected to stay flat or dip slightly versus 2026. Used ship purchases (S&P), however, depended heavily on one-off large deals (like Sinokor's VLCC purchase) in H1 2026, so if that deal effect fades, there's a real risk of a sharp drop back to the 2025 level — simply scaling up the H1 pace for the full-year target would be risky. Meanwhile, ship deliveries are set to rise sharply on an already-fixed orderbook basis (about +43.6% vs. the 2026 actual+planned total), but because Korean owners tend to keep the same classification society after delivery, this growth doesn't translate directly into more new class business. Still, a modest CAC growth target is worth considering, based on class maintenance and re-registration work.

| Year | Ships Ordered | Order Volume (thousand GT) | Order Volume (million GT) | Order Value ($bn) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2023 | 76 ships | 2,491 | 2.5 | — | Confirmed |
| 2024 | 96 ships | 4,500 | 4.5 | — | Confirmed |
| 2025 | 85 ships | **5,588** | **5.6** | **$7.9** | Confirmed (full year) |
| **2026 (Jan–Jun so far)** | **67 ships** | **3,047** | **3.0** | **$3.8** | Provisional, up **+9.0%** year-on-year |

> **2027 order forecast:** No credible source — including Clarkson — publishes an actual forecast for 2027 newbuilding orders. But given the 2026 H1 order pace (+9% year-on-year) and oversupply worries, the most likely outcome is that **2027 stays around the 2026 level (about 6,000 thousand GT on a yearly-rate basis) or dips slightly**.

**Checking the math (yearly-rate for new orders and the 2027 outlook):**

$$\text{2026 H1 actual scaled up to a full year: } 3{,}047 \times 2 = 6{,}094\text{ thousand GT} \approx 6.1\text{ million GT}$$

$$\text{2027 outlook: } \sim6{,}000\text{ thousand GT} \pm 5{\sim}10\% \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{flat or slightly lower vs. 2026}$$

> **Note:** No credible body publishes an actual order forecast. What does exist is the delivery schedule for ships already under contract (see Section 3 below).

## 3. Ship Deliveries: Checked Against Clarkson's Orderbook

### Confirmed Numbers (Source: Clarkson Research WFM Vol.17 No.7, July 2026) ³

Orderbook snapshot (as of July 1, 2026): **245 ships, 13.7 million GT** (16.4% of the fleet owned)

| Delivery Period | Volume (thousand GT) | Volume (million GT) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2026 H1 (actual) | 1,616 | 1.6 | Actually delivered (33 ships), down -21.0% year-on-year ⁴ |
| 2026 H2 (remaining, planned) | 1,100 | 1.1 | Scheduled per Clarkson's orderbook |
| **2026 full year (actual + planned)** | **2,716** | **2.7** | H1 actual + H2 planned |
| **2027** | **3,900** | **3.9** | Already-fixed schedule |
| 2028 | 4,500 | 4.5 | Already-fixed schedule |
| 2029 and later | 4,300 | 4.3 | Already-fixed schedule |

- **Why 2027 deliveries jump:** Ships ordered heavily during the post-pandemic boom (2020–2022) take 2–3 years to build, so a large batch is landing between 2026 and 2028. 2027 sees a bigger jump than 2026 because more of the ships in the current orderbook are scheduled to arrive that year.
- **Note:** Clarkson's orderbook-based delivery schedule only counts ships still left to deliver as of the snapshot date (July 1, 2026). So on its own, the "2026" figure only means the remaining H2 volume (1,100 thousand GT). Comparing that directly against the full-year 2027 figure would compare half a year against a full year and overstate the growth rate. To avoid that, we pulled the actual H1 2026 delivery figure (1,616 thousand GT) from a separate Clarkson table (Ownership of Newbuilding Deliveries) and added it in.

**Checking the math (2026 full-year actual+planned vs. 2027):**

$$\text{2026 full year (actual + planned)} = 1{,}616\text{ thousand GT} + 1{,}100\text{ thousand GT} = 2{,}716\text{ thousand GT}$$

$$\text{Rate of change} = \left(\frac{3{,}900-2{,}716}{2{,}716}\right) \times 100 \approx +43.6\%$$

**Result:** Korean shipowners' ship deliveries are set to rise from 2,716 thousand GT in 2026 (1,616 actual + 1,100 planned) to 3,900 thousand GT in 2027 — **about +43.6%** (based on actual deliveries plus the already-fixed orderbook schedule).

## 4. Used Ship Purchases (S&P): Actual Performance

Facing tight shipyard slots and high newbuilding costs, Korean shipowners have good reason to keep using the used-ship market. **H1 2026 saw the highest level of S&P activity on record.**

### Yearly S&P Actuals (Source: Clarkson Research WFM Vol.17 No.7, July 2026) ³

| Year | Ships Bought | Volume (thousand GT) | Volume (million GT) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2022 | 28 ships | 2,086 | 2.1 | Confirmed |
| 2023 | 25 ships | 1,476 | 1.5 | Confirmed |
| 2024 | 54 ships | **3,120** | **3.1** | Confirmed |
| 2025 | 39 ships | **3,226** | **3.2** | Confirmed (full year) |
| **2026 (Jan–Jun so far)** | **70 ships** | **10,180** | **10.2** | Up **+531.1%** year-on-year, **world's No.1** buyer |

> **Note:**

- **Why H1 2026 spiked:** Large owners like Sinokor concentrated big VLCC and tanker S&P deals, and demand exploded for eco-friendly used ships ahead of the 2027 IMO carbon rules.
- **Direction:** S&P activity could stay elevated in H2 2026, but the size of the H1 2026 spike mostly came from one-off large deals.

**Checking the math (S&P yearly-rate and the 2027 outlook):**

$$\text{2026 H1 actual scaled up to a full year (includes one-off deals, simple math): } 10{,}180 \times 2 = 20{,}360\text{ thousand GT} \approx 20.4\text{ million GT}$$

$$\text{Normalization scenario (excluding one-off deals, back to the usual 2025 level): } \approx 3{,}226\text{ thousand GT} \;\left(\frac{3{,}226-20{,}360}{20{,}360}\times100 \approx -84\%\right)$$

$$\text{2027 outlook: once the one-off deal effect fades} \;\rightarrow\; \textbf{could return to the 2025 level — risk of a sharp drop vs. 2026}$$
