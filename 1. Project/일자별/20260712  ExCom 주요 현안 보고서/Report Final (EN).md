# ExCom Key Issues Report — BV Korea (2026.07.12)

---

## 1. Best Practices

---

### ① BV's Largest-Ever Single CAC Contract: Sinokor, 63 Vessels

**Strategic Value**
- The largest single CAC/TOC contract in BV M&O history
- A growth structure that leads to follow-on CAP Survey revenue
- Delivered through collaboration between HQ's Global Tanker Segment, BV Korea, and multiple BV offices

---

## 2. Top Challenges

---

### Challenge ① KR's Structural Home-Ground Advantage

Korean shipowners default to KR (Korean Register) because of regulatory familiarity, relationship networks, and price competitiveness. For BV to win large accounts, it must offer pricing close to KR's — and this directly squeezes Korea office profitability.

---

## 3. Requests for ExCom/HQ Support and Change

---

### Proposal ① Formally Register Sinokor as a Key Account

**Background**
As of 2026, Sinokor is the world's largest VLCC owner, and through its 50:50 partnership with MSC, it controls 20–25% of the global VLCC market. BV Korea is already executing a 63-vessel TOC contract, and expanding into NC contracts is a strategic priority going forward.

**Request**
We ask ExCom to formally register Sinokor as a 2026 Key Account (KA), and to prioritize HQ resources — a dedicated Key Account manager, deployment of HQ technical experts, and Business Development support.

---

### Proposal ② Recognize Sales Office Profit Credit on Must-Win Accounts

**Request**
We request that BV's internal profit-sharing guidelines formally codify an "Account Owner contribution credit" principle, standardizing the profit split between the office performing the survey and the office that owns the account (i.e., allocating a set share of the sales contribution to Korea).

The Sinokor 63-vessel TOC contract was approved as a Must-Win project on an expected group-wide margin of roughly 18.8% (based on a 30-vessel TOC). However, most VLCC surveys are actually performed outside Korea, and to win the TOC survey fee contract, pricing was set close to KR's low rates. As a result, CD Pusan — the office that drove the sale — bears the full loss. Alex Gregg-Smith (M&O President) asked BV China/Singapore to cap their internal fees, but no structural solution has been put in place yet.

**Supporting Document** — Email from Alex Gregg-Smith (M&O President), dated 2026.01.16:

> *"BV has the opportunity to take the CAC of up to 30 VLCC which are in the process of being purchased by Sinokor... up to close to 5mGT, globally for NS surveys at Group level we should see a margin of approx 18.8% (Division fees neutralized) plus there are other opportunities for more profitable work (CAP survey) and also NC for further VLCC going forward...... it would be appreciated if **BV China and BV Singapore can give consideration to limit/cap their internal fees towards Korea**, in order to mitigate the expected local losses and share the burden."* — Alex Gregg-Smith, 2026.01.16
