Greek shipowner Evangelos Marinakis voiced confidence in the VLCC market despite a rising newbuilding orderbook.

Speaking at the TradeWinds Shipowners Forum, Marinakis also tipped his hat to the Sinokor-Aponte alliance, which made a historic move in the VLCC sector this year.

VLCC newbuildings have piled up in the last year, attracting both existing players and new or returning entrants to the market.

“If you check and analyse the profile of the world crude fleet, you see that it’s the oldest we have had in the last two decades,” Marinakis said.

“So, for sure, there is a need for new tonnage.”

He said the dark fleet, which he believes numbers over 1,000 ships, was a second supporting factor for tankers overall.

“If you see from one side the age profile, the other side the dark fleet, I think that for 2026, 2027, and even 2028, we need \[more\] ships,” Marinakis said.

“So in my opinion, if you have a newbuilding programme with prompt deliveries, you are well placed.

“And also, don’t forget that in our case we sold a significant amount of secondhand VLCCs in the last 18 months, and of course they were replaced with new ships, but much more environmentally friendly.”

Marinakis completed the largest shipping IPO for 20 years, taking Capital Tankers public in Oslo earlier in 2026.

It owns a fleet of 30 tankers, with 11 on the water and 19 under construction, for delivery between 2026 and 2028.

Capital Tankers also holds options for an additional 13 vessels, including 11 VLCCs.

Marinakis was also positive on the emergence of the new power in the VLCC market.

Sinokor Maritime’s push for big tankers in the sale-and-purchase and time-charter markets in late 2025 and into 2026 gave it a market share without precedent.

Estimates suggest the new partners have a share of around 25% of the compliant VLCC spot market.

“In my opinion, it was a very clever move,” Marinakis said.

The Greek owner said he calculated that it was possible to buy the trading VLCC fleet for about $60bn, reasoning there was no other business of such significance where it was possible to develop such a dominant market position.

“In my opinion, it was a brilliant move,” Marinakis said.

“The only thing is, how they will manage to trade the vessels from now on.

“I admit that it was a very clever move for someone who has a lot of cash and has taken advantage of a different market, like the container market.”

Gianluigi Aponte overtook Maersk Line as the world’s largest container line in the past five years and has since built a position controlling over one-fifth of the world’s container ships.

Marinakis is no stranger to large transactions, having completed a $1bn VLCC fleet sale to Bahri in 2024 and traded in and out of various markets in the past decade.
