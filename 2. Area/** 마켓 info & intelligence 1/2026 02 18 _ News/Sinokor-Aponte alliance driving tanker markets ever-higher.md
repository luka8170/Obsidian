DHT Holdings has confirmed a blockbuster VLCC time charter as the new Sinokor-Gianluigi Aponte tie-up continues to make it rain on tankers.

The New York-listed shipowner said on Wednesday that the 320,100-dwt DHT Opal (built 2012) was set to earn $90,000 per day on a year-long deal with a “global energy company”, confirming a TradeWinds report from Tuesday.

“The charterer is reported to be Koch — again showing that the Sinokor/Aponte stunt is making traditional charterers a bit anxious,” Pareto Securities head of research Eirik Haavaldsen said, referring to the duo’s shopping spree in which it has amassed a VLCC fleet with as many as 130 ships.

He added that “one could easily read this as an even stronger market data point” than [Okeanis Eco Tankers’ $91,140 per day deal](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/tankers/okeanis-eco-tankers-sets-huge-new-benchmark-with-vlcc-charter-to-koch/2-1-1944134) with Koch Shipping for the 300,300-dwt Nissos Nikouria (built 2022) announced on Monday.

Both ships are scrubber-fitted, but the DHT Opal is a decade older and lacks the Nissos Nikouria’s eco design.

Danske Bank said the [Sinokor-Aponte deals](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/containers/msc-linked-to-deal-for-30-container-ships-in-sinokor-fleet/2-1-1924429) have sent charterers “rushing to secure tonnage”, with time charter rates climbing from around $65,000 per day in December for a one-year deal to $78,300 per day now.

These takes echo what chief executive Svein Moxnes Harfjeld said during DHT Holdings’ fourth-quarter earnings call this month.

While not naming Sinokor or Aponte — instead calling them “[the aggregator](https://www.tradewindsnews.com/finance/-fundamental-shift-dht-becomes-first-vlcc-owner-to-allude-to-sinokor-aponte-goliath-in-earnings/2-1-1938973)” — he said their fleet now makes up around 25% of all VLCCs when dark fleet ships and non-spot-trading ships are stripped out.

Then, he touted a one-year VLCC fixture at $85,000 per day as an example of how high the market has gone.

The DHT Holdings and Okeanis Eco Tankers fixtures best broker estimates, with Clarksons’ highest one-year assessment — for a scrubber-fitted, eco-designed VLCC — reading at $81,750 per day last week.

MB Shipbrokers’ reading was $86,500 per day, up $11,500.

ABG Sundal Collier analyst Petter Haugen said the tanker market remains tight and that as long as Opec does not cut back production, the year is expected to be the strongest in some time.

But he said value was already priced in, with DHT Holdings trading at a price to net asset value ratio of 1.2 and Okeanis Eco Tankers at 1.3.

“We struggle to find sufficient upside at current prices,” Haugen added, reiterating “hold” ratings on both companies.

Danske Bank, which discussed the recent time charter strength in relation to Frontline, reiterated a “buy” rating.

It expects fourth-quarter consensus estimates to be a high bar to clear before the 27 February earnings release, but said recent rates should revise forecasts up.

DHT Holdings’ New York Stock Exchange-listed shares rose $0.36 to $16.27 on Tuesday and a further $0.24 in pre-market trading, while Okeanis Eco Tankers erased a $0.27 per share loss by adding $1.07 in pre-market trading to $43.78.

Frontline’s US-listed shares finished Tuesday up $0.60 to $31.52 and added $0.74 in premarket trading to $32.26.
