## 1. ESG / Green Strategy (Ship Resilience Index)

- **Key ideas explained**: "Eco-friendly" means cutting environmental impact right now, and it needs a certificate to prove it — it is more of a feeling/experience. "Sustainability" means making good choices over the whole life of the ship, for the next generation. "**Fuel economy**" (as opposed to simple fuel efficiency) means making the most revenue per ton of fuel used — by optimizing cargo, passengers, speed, and maintenance together. Panstar's fuel surcharge changes (up and down by small amounts) were mentioned as an example.
- **Panstar Miracle case study**: What guests actually notice affects the "green" image — smoke color when entering port (white/yellow/black smoke), whether the scrubber is working, and low-noise/low-vibration design.
- **Ship Resilience Index (new, development started about 2 months ago)**: A framework of Vulnerability → Impact → Absorption → Recovery. It scores the ship on 4 areas — **fuel, carbon, trade, and regulation** — using a checklist method, similar to a class survey. The goal is to give ship owners and banks a report showing how resilient (future-proof) the ship is.
- **Regulatory outlook**: Around 2027, new IMO rules on battery fire safety are expected (cargo separation, thermal monitoring, central fire detection), driven by more electric vehicles and battery cargo. Panstar Miracle is well prepared for Onshore Power Supply (OPS) thanks to its modular design. Japan is part of the Green Corridor group and is preparing related rules; BV is also involved in this working group.
- **Fuel flexibility**: The ship's tank coating already supports biofuel, methanol, and ammonia in addition to HFO/MGO. P&I clubs usually only allow fuel blends up to 30%; above that, extra risk assessment is needed. From a classification society's point of view, it is better to keep fuels in separate streams (e.g., injecting HFO and methanol separately) rather than mixing them in the tank.
- **Solar power / thermal energy**: Panstar has already installed solar panels on one high-speed craft and is monitoring power output live through an app (expansion is being considered). BV is co-developing a solar-to-electricity direct conversion system with a Dutch university (claimed efficiency about 19.2%, can store heat for about 4 days), but the maritime rules for this (about 9 IACS items) are not finished yet.
- **HVAC / sewage treatment**: Examples of energy savings include VFD (frequency control) on compressors, individual cabin temperature control, and UV-sterilized air conditioning, combined with a battery system (about 450 kW, under $100,000) for peak shaving. For sewage treatment, switching from the current vacuum system to a biological treatment system is being considered.
