## Slide 5 — Business Environment and Market Dynamics (Korea input)

### Market Dynamics
- Ship deliveries in Korea set to jump ~44% YoY to 3.9M GT in 2027 (vs. 2.7M GT in 2026), as vessels ordered in the 2020–2022 boom reach completion — a locked-in schedule, not a forecast
- New ship orders to stay flat or dip slightly (3.05M GT in H1 2026); Korean yards booked 3+ years out and prices remain high, so owners are in no rush to order more
- Used-ship (S&P) purchases should normalize back toward the 2025 level (~3.2M GT), after 2026's exceptional spike (10.2M GT in H1, Korea the world's #1 buyer) driven mainly by Sinokor's one-off VLCC acquisition

### Key Drivers
- Sinokor's 30-VLCC fleet win (CAC secured over KR) — Korea's single largest strategic account, anchoring class market share
- FYF pipeline conversion in progress: Ventus/Njord, Panstar, Hana Marine, Hyundai LNG Shipping — 4+ prospects in active negotiation
- NC newbuilding relationships building: HMM, Polaris VLOC, LNGC — early-stage but growing pipeline

### Challenges
- No dedicated Key Account Manager in Korea — one BD manager covers KA relationship management, FYF negotiation, NC pipeline, and HQ/APA coordination simultaneously
- Most Sinokor VLCC periodic surveys are executed in Singapore/China (APA), not Korea — Korea's revenue capture is limited without a formalized internal fee-sharing credit
- KR is actively defending its position on Sinokor and other core accounts — risk of erosion without deeper C-suite engagement

### Opportunities
- The +44% delivery surge creates class maintenance, re-registration, and CAP survey demand tied to Korea-originated newbuildings
- Converting the FYF pipeline (4 active prospects) would materially grow the recurring NS revenue base
- Sinokor's CAP survey program can be used as a retention/stickiness lever during the fleet's rapid expansion
