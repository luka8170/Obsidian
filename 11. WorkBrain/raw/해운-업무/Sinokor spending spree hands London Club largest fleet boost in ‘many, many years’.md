Senior executive says the International Group member had an increase in tonnage across shipping sectors

![London P&I Club chief underwriting officer Reto Toggwiler.](https://image.dngroup.com/global/nhst/binary/6cfb86b9d98d6b6cdc117bb40549029f?crop=1320%2C880%2Cx0%2Cy63%2Csafe&width=1000&format=auto&quality=80)

Published 3 March 2026, 21:18

Tanker-hungry Sinokor Maritime’s spending spree contributed to the largest tonnage increase for years for protection and indemnity insurer the London Club.

The London Club reported a 14.5% year-on-year increase of tonnage on its books, helped by securing a share of Sinokor’s growing fleet, chief underwriting officer Reto Toggwiler said.

The London Club is one of a number of clubs that provide cover for ships for the South Korean shipowner, which has bought more than 40 VLCCs since December to become a dominant force in that market.

Toggwiler said the club’s growth had come from owners of all sizes covering different sectors.

He said the timing of the Sinokor deals means that the club expects to add tonnage in the year to February 2027.

“They have been with us for three years, and we’ve got a large amount of tonnage from them,” Toggwiler said.

“As part of the expansion plan, they are sharing it with all of their holding clubs, and it’s quite significant.

“There’s a lot of tonnage coming, and it’s coming in tranches. The majority is coming as and when acquisitions come through.”

Toggwiler said there was a “buzz” around the club under new chief executive James Bean and newer chairman Vassilis J Laliotis, who took over at the beginning of the new policy year in February.

Toggwiler said the extra tonnage contributed to the “largest increase we have seen for many, many years”.

He said it came when rates were comparatively high and sustainable during a period of high claims.

“It’s a club in growth mode across most markets,” Toggwiler said.

The London Club, one of the smaller members of the 12-strong International Group of P&I Clubs, reported heavy underwriting losses over seven years to 2023-2024.

It responded by increasing premiums and stripping out fleets that had led to losses for the club.

Figures issued by the group showed an increase in tonnage on its mutual book from 49.5m gt to 56.7m gt in the year to 20 February.
